# Letter to public body

Reference guidance for correspondence with a public body that is not a venue: the police, a provincial or federal ministry, or a municipal office such as bylaw enforcement. Such a body receives the letter but prescribes no form or procedure for it. A body that does (a tribunal, a regulator with a published intake form) is a venue, and its correspondence is that venue's own kind.

How every letter is drafted, checked on its mockup and sent is `references/letters.md`. This file holds this audience's guidance only, and every item in it is the firm's own practice (`source: practitioner`).

---

## Core Principles

**The reader is an official, not an adversary.** The person who opens the letter has a caseload, a mandate and limited time. They act on letters that are specific, documented and easy to route. They set aside letters that are vague, emotional or long.

**Tone baseline:** Formal, factual, courteous and firm. State what happened and what you ask the body to do. Do not editorialize, and do not characterize anyone's motives.

**Every letter may be disclosed.** A public body's file can be produced to the person complained about, or released on an access request. Write nothing you would not want that person, or a judge, to read.

**Any legal proposition needs research first.** If the letter states what a bylaw, statute or rule of conduct requires, that statement goes through `/legal-research` before it appears in the draft.

---

## Structure

1. **Identification.** Who you are, who you act for, and the matter in one sentence.
2. **The request in one line.** What you are asking the body to do: investigate, enforce, respond, produce records, or note a complaint on file. Put it near the top so the letter is routed correctly.
3. **The facts.** Dates, addresses, file or occurrence numbers, and what was observed and by whom. Keep the facts in date order, and keep them separate from any opinion.
4. **Enclosures.** List each one (photographs, prior correspondence, notices received) and refer to it where it supports a fact.
5. **The response sought and by when.** Name the step you expect and a specific calendar date. Where the matter is urgent, say why in one sentence.
6. **Contact.** One direct contact for the reply, and the body's own reference number if one has been assigned.

---

## Complaints and Requests for Enforcement

- Quote the body's own file or occurrence number if a prior report was made.
- Describe the conduct complained of with particulars (when, where, how often), not conclusions.
- Ask for a specific step and a confirmation that the complaint has been received and assigned.
- Do not ask the body to take sides in the civil dispute. Ask it to exercise its own mandate.

## Requests for Records or Information

- Identify the records precisely: date range, location, record type and any reference number.
- If a formal access request process applies, say so and follow it rather than asking informally.

## Follow-Up Letters

- Refer to the earlier letter by date and to any reference number assigned.
- Restate the request in one line and set a new date.
- Stay courteous. A record of reasonable, documented follow-up is worth more than an escalation in tone.

---

## What to Avoid

- **Using a complaint as leverage in the civil matter.** Never link a report to the body with a demand on the other side.
- **Overstatement.** Do not call conduct "illegal" or "criminal" unless that conclusion has been researched and is necessary to the request.
- **Advocacy for the client's whole case.** Give the body only what it needs to act on its own mandate.
- **Copying the other side without a decision.** Whether the opposing party or counsel is copied is a choice the practitioner makes on each letter.

---

## Checklist Before Sending

- [ ] Confirmed the body is not a venue (it prescribes no form or procedure for this letter)
- [ ] The request appears in the first paragraph and is specific
- [ ] Facts are dated, particular and supportable, and separate from opinion
- [ ] Every enclosure is listed and referred to
- [ ] A response date is stated as a calendar date
- [ ] No legal proposition appears unless it has been through `/legal-research`
- [ ] Nothing in the letter links the report to a civil demand
- [ ] The letter reads well if disclosed to the person it concerns
