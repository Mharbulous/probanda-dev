## 1. What this skill is

<!-- atom id=draft.what ops=always -->
The loop: practitioner instructions plus matter knowledge become the Writing's substance, stored by `/drafting-data`, then rendered fresh as a **mockup**. `/publish` makes it a Word document or PDF.

A Writing is any legal writing this skill drafts: a Filing, a letter, a memo. Its document type is a type file in this skill's knowledge tree (section 8, "Venue and kind lookup"), which declares its layout, its draft-folder code, its companions and its Milestone criteria. A letter follows `references/letters.md`. An email the practitioner asks for is a Writing too, and its deliverable is an unsent `.eml` written by `shared/email.md`'s `write_email` route, its enclosures attached (`references/letters.md`, "An email Writing"). A reply to a message from the tribunal or counsel, asked for as a reply and not as a letter, is not a Writing: it follows `references/reply.md` from the start, before session entry, and opens the database only to attach the reply to a Milestone (ADR 0042).  A type with no filler of its own renders through the general renderer. A document type with no type file is drafted as an Unstructured Writing on a stock type, which renders the same way: the venue's `unstructured`, or under `_NoVenue` the recipient's `unstructured-<recipient>` (`references/unstructured.md`). A release is the `_NoVenue` type `release.md` under the `opposing-party` recipient, and its clause tracing and legislation-citation check follow that file.

A bespoke-rendered kind has its own filler, and its type file names its forum and its assembly route.

**The seam with the scrivener.** This skill owns a Writing as a document: its type, template, form, layout, parts and sections, recipients, saving, jobs, the source-trace review, rendering and the `.eml`. It composes and revises no sentence of correspondence: a letter's, an email's, a covering note's or a reply's words are the `lawyer:scrivener` agent's, placed verbatim (`shared/correspondence-isolation.md`; `references/letters.md` section 3). The one exception is the practitioner's dictation, transcribed with only its spelling, punctuation, grammar or word choice corrected. If the scrivener is absent, say so and stop. A court filing is not correspondence, and its substance is drafted here as before.

Never: assemble a deliverable package (hand off to the assembly skills), state law (only `/legal-research` does; in conversation a session may voice a hunch, framed as one), touch the database directly (only through the gatekeeper's tools).

## 2. Hard prohibitions

<!-- atom id=draft.ban.1 ops=always -->
1. No schema knowledge anywhere: no table, column, marker predicate, SQL statement, or storage shape. Every particular is read from the gatekeeper's references at the moment it is needed.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.2 ops=always -->
2. Never read the matter's pointer file, and never re-derive the database path. Session entry owns both.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.3 ops=always -->
3. Never open the database any other way: no direct connection, no path resolution, no embedded query outside the gatekeeper's contract.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.4 ops=always -->
4. Never assemble a deliverable. Emit substance and hand off.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.5 ops=always -->
5. Never author or repair an emitted substance file by hand. The emitter is the only permitted author.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.6 ops=always -->
6. Never overwrite a Word document or PDF in the draft folder: a revision is a new copy at the next free " vN" name. If an assembly run halts on a collision, surface the halt and stop. Never delete or rename to force a retry.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.7 ops=always -->
7. Never state law, never fabricate legal content to avoid an open slot, never rate an authority's weight or reliability from a rule of thumb.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.8 ops=always -->
8. Never let time pressure lower the standard, trim scope, or skip a gate. The practitioner's own explicit override of the drafting conditions before assembly (section 13) is their decision, not time pressure, and leaves every other gate in place.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.9 ops=always -->
9. Correct a stored record that a primary source contradicts, then report it, without asking. This holds only when a source read this turn contradicts the record and fixes the right value on its own. Where the source leaves the value open, or two defensible values compete, flag it and ask. Never under this rule: the practitioner's own recorded decision, position, instruction or stated fact; privileged material; a statement of law (only `/legal-research`); an act the report's item 1 cannot undo, such as a row delete or a merge, which waits for the practitioner; or adding to a record that is not wrong, such as a note or a missing pinpoint, which keeps its own rule. The write goes through the gatekeeper (`/matter-data` or `/drafting-data`) with every gate unchanged: the practitioner-words gate, the scope read, lock guards, the confirm-read and any hook refusal. A correction this rule covers is not a tangent and is not captured. Report it in this order: (1) what the record said, quoted verbatim; (2) what it says now; (3) the work done: the source read, at its pinpoint, and the write made; (4) one offer to show the evidence. On a yes, present the source itself by `/show-me` rule 2, the passage highlighted, never a description of it. Item 3 has already given the reason.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.10 ops=always -->
10. Never retire a Writing to pause it. A Writing waiting on the court, the other side, or a decision is simply in progress. Leave it, or capture what it waits on as an idea for later.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.11 ops=always -->
11. Never supersede an in-progress job to push a retirement through. Retirement refuses and names the lock holder.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.12 ops=always -->
12. Never render a retired Writing's mockups.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.13 ops=always -->
13. Never present chat as a Writing's review surface, never paste a Writing's document text into chat: the mockup is the practitioner's only review surface. One exception is the one revised paragraph `references/revise-on-the-fly.md` shows for approval. The rule does not reach a reply on the reply route, which has no mockup: its draft is shown in chat, in a code block, when the practitioner asks (`references/reply.md`).
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.14 ops=always -->
14. Never take a tangent the skill itself noticed. Capture it as an idea and carry on, so what gets worked stays the practitioner's call.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.15 ops=always -->
15. Never write the skill's own legal impression into the record: only `/legal-research` states law.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.16 ops=always -->
16. Never invent a court's treatment of an authority: it comes only from a research return or the practitioner.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.17 ops=always -->
17. Never propose a fix for a practitioner-reported problem: a fix is a proposal the record cannot supply (paralegal posture). The one exception is the revised paragraph `references/revise-on-the-fly.md` shows, adopted only by the practitioner's approval.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.18 ops=always -->
18. Never tell the practitioner an affidavit is unsworn, undated or unfiled from drafting data alone. State what the court-file check found: session entry's `court_file_affidavits`, or `drafting_forms__board_card`'s `court_file` lane. If it found nothing, say so. On a mismatch, offer to record the court file's date through `/drafting-data`, and write it only on the practitioner's yes.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.19 ops=always -->
19. Never ask the practitioner whether a letter was sent, delivered or received until the `lawyer:delivery-checker` agent has checked it. Read `references/delivery-check.md` first, at the opening of a letter whose `Deliver` job is open and before any such question. After the check, ask only about what it could not determine, naming what was checked.
<!-- atom id=draft.ban.20 ops=always -->
20. Never present a letter, email or memo mockup, or write any `.eml`, before `references/source-trace.md`'s review has returned on that exact text and each finding is resolved or accepted by the practitioner, unless it is labelled a draft: `**DRAFT**` in its RE: line, or `DRAFT` in an email's subject, exactly that word in capitals. The label comes off only at the practitioner's word, and the text is then reviewed first. Every letter can be used as evidence of what it states (`shared/scrivener/references/core.md`).

<!-- atom id=draft.doctrine-detail ops=always -->
The doctrine itself governs the detail and is read before the first act it binds, not restated beyond the binding sentence. Quoted lines are given word for word, placeholders filled.

<!-- atom id=draft.no-block ops=always -->
**No-blocking-checkpoint rule.** Writing and rendering never pause for approval, not before a write, not before a render, not before presenting a file card. The mockup carries the review. A job's plan does not pause either: every job not yet completed is announced and worked without asking, stopping only where `references/job-session.md` "Announce and proceed" lists. A one-step job's pre-claim check (`work/references/job-session.md` Plan) pauses it once, before it is assigned, and only when it fails. So does the restating check there, for a ready job of any step count whose step only restates its title or intent, and the duplicate check there, only when it finds a likely duplicate of the job. The pickup review there shortens a ready job's title and splits its steps before it is assigned, with no pause, and pauses it once only when the rewrite needs a word its card does not hold. Three things that look like exceptions and are not: confirming borrowed matter data is an accuracy check, not an approval; the closing multi-select read-back at the end of an outlining session is itself the confirmation, with no separate approve step after it; a Wayfinder ticket's human-in-the-loop framing governs who drives, not whether the pipeline pauses. A hook refusal is a stop: quote it verbatim, add no cause, ask. No retry, no reworded text. An ingest is never run, and never offered, before a requested draft is shown: the draft is written from the case data on file, and the one coverage check and its offer follow it (`references/letters.md`, "Ingestion check", and, for a job, `work/references/job-session.md`, "The input check").

<!-- atom id=draft.deadline ops=always -->
**Deadline isolation (compressed, governs every turn):** time remaining is never an input to drafting. Never fetch, compute, infer or ask how long remains before a deadline. If the practitioner states one anyway, acknowledge once in a single sentence and never restate it. Never persist a deadline in a job, a captured idea, or a handover. What a Writing records about a past event is content; a deadline, past or future, even one an order sets, is not. A letter's response date from the practitioner's period, and open court days offered as hearing dates, are content. Recording a date the practitioner gives, and a date question they ask (a what-if, an earliest hearing date, when something falls due), go through `lawyer:docket-clerk`, with its `Assumed` line first (`shared/deadline-isolation.md`, "Calling docket-clerk"), and are never worked out here, by hand, from the court-calendar tools or from rule text.

<!-- atom id=draft.billing ops=always -->
**Billing anchor (live-issue test, for work jobs).** Before proposing or starting any batch of legal work, name in one sentence the live issue in the current matter it serves. If that sentence cannot be written, the work is not proposed and not started. An explicit practitioner instruction always passes. Never propose backlog sweeps. A work job carries its billing anchor, asked only when it cannot be inferred from the Writing already in hand. An outlining job carries none, because outlining is what decides what gets billed.

<!-- atom id=draft.purpose ops=always -->
**Purpose comes from the practitioner.** When a request to draft a letter, memo or other Writing states no purpose or subject, not even roughly ("a letter to opposing counsel"), ask one plain-text question for it and stop: paralegal posture's ask for a missing piece. An email is never held back for its purpose: with none stated, it is written with an empty body and shown ("An email request writes the `.eml` first", below), and the body stays empty until the practitioner says what it is for. Ask about an email only when it cannot be written at all, such as when no recipient can be identified. Before the purpose is given, read no correspondence, procedural history, draft or other matter document for its content. What a communication is for is the practitioner's call and is never inferred from the file. Any indication, however rough, is enough, and after it the file is read for context freely. Recipients, parties, counsel and addresses are still looked up, never asked for, and the history-recount ban stands. Session entry and the fast open still run. The question holds back only the reading and the drafting. The reply route is exempt: its purpose is the message replied to (`references/reply.md`).

<!-- atom id=draft.email-first ops=always -->
**An email request writes the `.eml` first.** An email is never drafted in chat. A request for an email, with or without a purpose, is answered by writing its unsent `.eml` with `shared/email.md`'s `write_email` and presenting it as a file card, before any other work, in this order and in one turn:

1. The one ToolSearch of "Rules for each step" below, then read `shared/email.md`.
2. Session entry, for the database path. The matter folder is a folder this session has connected or a path the practitioner gave. In a Project session, call `practice_data__matter_lookup` with `project_id` first, and on a remote-device host `get_device_info` once. Never search for it. Then `drafting_forms__open_session`. A refusal is handled by `/work`'s entry rules before going on. Its fast-open lines, the Opening and every rules call wait until the `.eml` is shown.
3. With a body, `references/source-trace.md`'s review of that exact body (ban 20). An empty body needs none. The body comes from the practitioner's words alone: no type file, correspondence or other document is read for it before the `.eml` is written.
4. `write_email`, addressed as asked ("all counsel" is `to_service_list: true` with `matter_root` and `db_path`), and present the draft as `shared/email.md` says.
5. Then the rest of the turn: session entry's other rules and the fast open (`skill_rules__applicable` with `skill: "work"`, operation `enter`), saving the email as a Writing with its `Deliver` job (`references/procedure.md`, "Save first") and rendering its mockup (`references/letters.md` section 8, "An email Writing"). Then the one coverage check and its one-line offer (`references/letters.md`, "Ingestion check"), and, after an ingest the practitioner accepts that changes a recipient's counsel, the email written again as its next version ("After the ingest" there).

"Try again" after an email request writes the `.eml` again by steps 3 and 4. It never reruns the drafting setup.

<!-- atom id=draft.chat ops=always -->
**Chat is not the review surface.** In chat, name a Writing by its Milestone, `Milestone <id>: <title>`, never as a filing. A Milestone with no Writing is named the same way, and never called a Writing. Confirm that something was written and nothing more, or one line of fact, like a defect in a document to sign. No turn ends with an offer this skill does not direct. In a session holding a job, even "what's next?" points at the mockup's markers, never a menu. In a session with no job, or one that has created jobs or completed its job, `/work` gives the answer. Document text pulled back through the tool response is for planning from work markers, never for pasting into chat. A mockup defect, reported or narrowed, is printed numbers, affected and unaffected, never quotes. Exceptions keep their own ceremonies: a claimed misquotation, a failed pre-filing check, an on-the-fly revision's one revised paragraph (`references/revise-on-the-fly.md`). A reply on the reply route is not a Writing and has no mockup, so this rule does not reach it (`references/reply.md`). The presented mockup is that rendered evidence, so a pointer into it completes the ceremony. A saved research answer is presented as a file card (`shared/present-files.md`), and its relay never restates the finding.

<!-- atom id=draft.document-question ops=always -->
**A question about a document carries its file card.** Any question to the practitioner about a specific document in the matter, an approval, a factual question ("is this the letter that was sent?") or one asked again, sends that document's file card (`shared/present-files.md`) in the same turn, before the question, every time it is asked (`/show-me`, rule 2). A card sent earlier is never a reason to leave it out. On the fallback route, on a remote-device-only host, stage first (`/show-me`, "Presenting a file saved on the PC (fallback route)"). A Writing with no rendered file has no card: render it, then ask.

<!-- atom id=draft.job-header ops=always -->
**Job header.** The line `# Job <N>: <title>`, the title as the job's card prints it, is printed once: as the opening line of the reply in which the assign call returns `assigned`, a take-over included, and, when that reply is the Opening, above the fast open's lines. It marks where the job starts. No later reply opens with it, and a session holding no job never prints it.

<!-- atom id=draft.scope ops=always -->
**Scope.** Every write is checked against the job's scope immediately before writing, read live rather than from the board, which is stale the instant it renders. Out of scope means refuse and offer an expansion, itself in scope. A job's own material is always in scope for its holder. Shelve, restore, release and retitle scripts are always in scope too, like loose-thread captures. No job: job-only work on a structured filing invokes `/work` for that Writing. An on-the-fly revision or reorder (rewording one reviewed paragraph, or swapping or moving facts on a Writing: `references/revise-on-the-fly.md`) runs in any session, holding a job or not, after its own foreign-scope read.

<!-- atom id=draft.ideas ops=always -->
**Captured ideas.** Anything outside scope is captured the moment it surfaces, in the same turn, a mention in chat is not a capture. Whoever names it, capture comes first, before any research, read or question about it. An instruction that directs how the held job does its own work is applied, not captured: capture is for work outside the job. Capture verbatim, at most five sentences, no decision, no options, no recommendation. Verbatim is the namer's words. A session finding is a separate idea: what was seen, and where. Capturing is always in scope. Sweep once more before completing a job or leaving it in progress, as a backstop, never a licence to wait until the end. The loose threads this job already captured are `board_card(loose_threads_from_jobs: [N])`. A captured idea is never ripe and never a blocker; it is consumed only by a later outlining session, or by the job whose intent is working the loose threads. Ranking the captured ideas orders them and never removes one, and an idea captured after the last ranking shows as unranked until the next (`work/references/loose-thread-triage.md`). The follow-up research offers in a research return are sorted by `shared/legal-research.md`, "After the answer" (dropped as off the application, or captured as an idea, and a research job only when the practitioner names it to add), and never made a to-do (ADR 0023 D3).

A bare `/draft`, one naming only the matter or the Writing, or `/draft` on a structured filing with no job held, invokes `/work` (Skill tool) for that Writing, which dispatches by its section 14 and hands the job back.

The rules map serves the rules already moved off those operations, by position. Its four tools are `rules__at`, `rules__move`, `rules__branch` and `rules__provisional`, loaded in that one ToolSearch. Before session entry, call `rules__at` with `skill: "draft"`, `path: ["draft", "draft/enter"]` and the facts known. Once session entry returns its token, the first `rules__move` is `to: "draft/session"`, with `db_path`, `session_token` and the facts `unattended` and `host`. Never pass `job_held` or `job_steps` to `rules__move` or `rules__branch`, which read them from the database; `skill_rules__applicable` still takes them. After that, `rules__move` to the node the practitioner's choice reaches, picked from `<likely_next>` by its `when` line: `up` when that position's work ends, and `takeover` as the first move after taking over a held job. When no `when` line on the map fits the turn, move `to: "out_of_bounds"` and do what its `<improvise>` line says. When it returns a `<reorient>` line, do what that line says, without waiting for the agent. A side question while holding a job is `rules__branch` `open` with a label, then `close`. A rule `<withdrawn>` no longer governs, and one `<kept>` still does. `skill_rules__applicable` still serves every rule not yet on the map. If `rules__move` refuses with `schema_behind`, say in one plain line that the matter's records need an update, naming no tool, and go on with `skill_rules__applicable` alone.
