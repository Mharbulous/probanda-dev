# Drafting habits that put an unsourced fact in a letter

Read when: running or acting on the source-trace review (`references/source-trace.md`). Each entry is a habit a model drafting correspondence falls into, with its id, which the review's findings use. The drafter's own overstatement of a source is `references/overstatement.md`'s and is not repeated here.

Every letter can be used as evidence of what it states. Each habit below makes the firm state, as its own fact, something no primary source it has read supports.

**`adopted_characterization`.** Another party's or other counsel's account of events is written as fact. Example: other counsel writes "your client ignored three requests", and the draft says "you did not respond to three requests". Fix: attribute it ("Ms. Y's counsel says …"), or check it against the requests themselves.

**`embellished_source`.** The draft adds a detail the source never states: a name, a date, a number, a reason. Example: the source says "the adjuster called", and the draft says "Ms. Lee, the adjuster, called". Fix: cut the added detail, or find a source that states it.

**`recharacterized_statement`.** A statement is reworded into something stronger, weaker or different in kind. Example: "confirms you are setting the hearing for 4 May" becomes "agrees to the hearing on 4 May". Fix: keep the source's own verb and strength, or quote it.

**`relayed_report`.** A third party's report of a fact that a primary record could settle is passed on as fact unchecked. Example: another firm writes "the response was filed", and the draft tells the client it was filed. Fix: check the filed copy or the court record, or attribute it ("X's office says it was filed; we have not yet seen the filed copy").

**`contradicted_record`.** The draft states something an email or document already on file contradicts. Example: the draft says a witness can only be reached by phone, while an earlier email in the mailbox gives that witness's email address. Fix: correct the statement from the record.

**`unanswered_characterization`.** A reply leaves unanswered a characterization in the message it answers, where silence can be read as agreement. Example: opposing counsel's letter says "as your client admitted", and the reply addresses only the scheduling. Fix: ask the practitioner whether to answer it. Never answer it silently on their behalf.
