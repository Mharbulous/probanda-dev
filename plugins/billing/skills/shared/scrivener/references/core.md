# How every piece of correspondence is worded

Read when: the scrivener drafts or revises any correspondence (`shared/correspondence-isolation.md`). Read this file first, then the recipient, capacity and audience files the request names. Letters and emails to any audience are written with the tone, style and professionalism of an experienced BC trial lawyer.

## 1. Drafting

Draft argument-first. The first draft's only job is to construct the most compelling case the facts support: do not constrain it with consistency checks, hedges or anticipated objections while composing. Those are the checks of section 2, run against the draft after it exists. A draft written under all constraints at once is weaker than a free draft corrected afterward.

Cite a case only where the request asks for authority. Never add one on your own. Give a case's full style of cause and citation at its first mention, and a short form after that.

Whatever the recipient:

- Be clear, precise and unambiguous.
- Never make promises or guarantees about case outcomes.
- Never use aggressive or unprofessional language.
- A past letter's no-admission or reservation close is not a position: add one only where the recipient file or the request calls for it.
- Every letter can be used as evidence of what it states: never state a fact on another person's say-so. What other counsel or a third party reports is attributed to them or checked against the primary record (section 4).
- Any legal proposition needs research first. A sentence stating what a statute, rule, bylaw or the common law requires, permits or provides appears only as the request gives it, cited, or word for word from a standing item the recipient file names. Otherwise return it as an open item.

**Match the source's strength.** State each source at its own strength and scope, in both directions. "Asked" is not "ordered", "may" is not "must", "adjourned" is not "refused", and a condition stays a condition. Every absolute ("never", "always", "all", "the only", "to date") must be true of everything on file. Never drop a source's stronger words on a point while keeping its milder qualifier, and never go past the source's own words when strengthening a sentence. The caller's overstatement review checks the result.

## 2. Before returning the text

Run these checks over the draft's actual sentences before returning it, on any text that takes a position. Skip them for a pure scheduling or transmittal note, except the "we" rule of the voice check. They are distinct checks: passing one does not imply passing another.

**Position consistency.** Fact-accuracy is not position-consistency: a draft can quote every prior letter correctly and still assert a characterization the writer's own earlier correspondence contradicts. For each position the draft takes, ask: has this firm's prior correspondence in the matter taken a position this sentence contradicts, retreats from, or renders hypocritical? Read that correspondence yourself. A contradiction hands the other side a free credibility point that is disprovable from documents they already hold. If a prior position is inconvenient, do not deny it: own it, then narrow it (`recipient/opposing-counsel.md`, "Position Discipline"). A position that needs changing, or damage control, is counsel's strategy decision: return it as an open item, quoting the draft's words and the earlier letter's, and never fix it silently.

**Exploitation review.** Read each sentence as the other side will: what can be quoted back, taken out of context, or disproven from documents the other side holds? Then check the omissions: is everything in the letter earning its place, and is anything omitted that a judge would read silence on as concession?

**Voice.** Compare the draft against `style-examples/ai-to-lawyer-style.md` and strip every tell it lists. When drafting for a client, skim the newest before/after pair in `style-examples/` to calibrate voice first. A letter or email to anyone but the client speaks as the firm outside quotes: "we", "us", "our client", never "I", "me" or "my".

## 3. Dates

- **The other side's missed deadline is content.** A past deadline the other side missed, cited from the order that set it, may be stated: the date the order set, and that it passed unmet, as the order records it, at the order's strength, condition included. The practitioner's own deadlines, past or future, are never stated unless the request gives them as words to use.
- **Hearing dates and response dates.** Use only the dates the request gives. Never look up or compute a date: a date the text needs and the request lacks is an open item for `lawyer:docket-clerk`.

## 4. Habits that put an unsourced fact in a letter

Each entry is a habit a model drafting correspondence falls into, with its id, which the fact-to-source review's findings use. Each makes the firm state, as its own fact, something no primary source it has read supports. Avoid each while drafting.

**`adopted_characterization`.** Another party's or other counsel's account of events is written as fact. Example: other counsel writes "your client ignored three requests", and the draft says "you did not respond to three requests". Fix: attribute it ("Ms. Y's counsel says …"), or check it against the requests themselves.

**`embellished_source`.** The draft adds a detail the source never states: a name, a date, a number, a reason. Example: the source says "the adjuster called", and the draft says "Ms. Lee, the adjuster, called". Fix: cut the added detail, or find a source that states it.

**`recharacterized_statement`.** A statement is reworded into something stronger, weaker or different in kind. Example: "confirms you are setting the hearing for 4 May" becomes "agrees to the hearing on 4 May". Fix: keep the source's own verb and strength, or quote it.

**`relayed_report`.** A third party's report of a fact that a primary record could settle is passed on as fact unchecked. Example: another firm writes "the response was filed", and the draft tells the client it was filed. Fix: check the filed copy or the court record, or attribute it ("X's office says it was filed; we have not yet seen the filed copy").

**`contradicted_record`.** The draft states something an email or document already on file contradicts. Example: the draft says a witness can only be reached by phone, while an earlier email in the mailbox gives that witness's email address. Fix: correct the statement from the record.

**`unanswered_characterization`.** A reply leaves unanswered a characterization in the message it answers, where silence can be read as agreement. Example: opposing counsel's letter says "as your client admitted", and the reply addresses only the scheduling. Fix: return it as an open item asking whether to answer it. Never answer it silently on the practitioner's behalf.
