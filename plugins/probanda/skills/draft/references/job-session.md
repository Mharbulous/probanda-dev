## Branch A: a job is chosen

### Assign

<!-- atom id=draft.assign ops=dispatch -->
A ready job is assigned to this session by one call, `drafting_data__assign_job` `{db_path, ticket_id, session_token}`, the token being the one session entry returned. The call runs the guarded assigning write (queued, unlocked, not shelved) and the confirming read and returns `assigned`, `lost_race`, `before_bf` or `no_match`. A ready job on a Writing whose type declares ingest inputs takes the input check (Plan, below) first, whatever its step count, and a ready one-step job then takes the pre-claim check (Plan, below). Each job is assigned only on results that say to. On `assigned`, add the job's started entry to `ProceduralHistory.md` (`shared/procedural-history.md`).
<!-- atom id=draft.assign.availability ops=input_check -->
The `Availability letter` stock job sits on a court filing, so its input check covers that filing's folders (court file and discovery), not a letter's.

<!-- atom id=draft.assign.review-jobs ops=counsel_review,scrutiny_review -->
A counsel's review job, whose title starts with `Counsel's review of `, is assigned the same way. Its one step is the practitioner's, so once it is assigned `references/counsel-review.md` runs in place of dispatch. A scrutiny review job, whose title starts with `Review scrutiny finding: `, is assigned the same way, and `references/authorities.md` "Failed scrutiny" runs in place of dispatch.

<!-- atom id=draft.assign.refused ops=assign_refused -->
**What the confirming read can show.** `lost_race`, another session's token, is a lost race: the job was just taken. Re-render and restate the Opening with one line saying so, and never re-run the assigning write to win it. A job the session chose itself is followed by a fresh choice from that board (`references/dispatch.md` section 14, Dispatch). `before_bf` means the job is held until its bring-forward date, the result's `bf_on`: nothing was written. Say so in one line, giving the date as a plain date with no day count, assign nothing in its place unless the practitioner names another job, and never offer to change or clear the date, which the practitioner alone asks for (`/drafting-data` §10b). `no_match`, no holder at all, is not a race — the update matched no row — so stop, assign nothing else, and report it. Either the board was stale and the job has since moved out of what the write's guards accept, or the write was composed wrong. Re-render before saying which; the result's `status` and `rejected_at` say which guard refused. In an unattended run (`references/dispatch.md` section 14) either result stops the run and goes in its report; there is no Opening to restate.

<!-- atom id=draft.assign.in-progress ops=take_over,route_reply -->
An In Progress job with every step stamped takes `references/dispatch.md` section 14's close form instead of this one.

An in-progress job named at the Opening takes the take-over form in `references/take-over.md`, which also holds the read-back of a taken-over, restored or released job.

### Plan

**The premise check.** A job's premise is its Proposal keys and nothing else: not the intent prose, not the steps, which restate the same keys, and not the scope entries, which are live-read gated where they are used. Right after the job is assigned and before any read-back, subtract from the assigned job's own keys every key that still derives: on the job's `keys:` line on its card, `board_card(jobs: [N])`, a key still derives when its `(now: <glyph> "<current item>")` names a Proposal, in any lane, and is dead when it reads `(now: dead)`. The card reads the inventory the Opening's render saved, so there is no second render and no second predicate pass. Read the job's `keys:` line, never board_card's Proposed lane: that lane hides a Proposal while a queued or in-progress job holds its key, and the assigned job holds its own keys by construction.

<!-- atom id=draft.premise.holds ops=plan_readback,take_over when=premise:holds -->
| What the subtraction leaves | What the practitioner is told |
|---|---|
| Nothing, every key still derives | One line in the header: each item's marker still fires. The work proceeds (Announce and proceed, below), unless a step only restates the title (below). |
<!-- atom id=draft.premise.failed ops=plan_readback,take_over when=premise:failed -->
| One or more keys | Name those items in your own visible reply, then take the Change path. Never proceed as stated. |
<!-- atom id=draft.premise.none ops=plan_readback,take_over when=premise:none -->
| No keys at all | Outlined by hand; no marker premise to check. |
<!-- atom id=draft.premise.mixed ops=plan_readback,take_over -->
| Keys, plus work described by hand | Report the keyed part and name the rest as unchecked. |

<!-- atom id=draft.premise.wording ops=premise_check -->
Word the two causes differently: a dead key is "the marker no longer fires on N of M items", a reworded item is "the item now reads: <new text>". A reworded item alone is reported in the header, never treated as moved, and the work proceeds as stated.

<!-- atom id=draft.premise.texts ops=premise_check,pre_claim -->
**Where both texts come from.** A key's item has changed when its stored text differs from its current text. The stored side is the quoted item after `<key> =` on the job's `keys:` line. The current side is the quoted item after the glyph in that key's `(now: <glyph> "<current item>")` on the same line, never read from the Proposed lane, as above. The "item now reads" report prints the current side. Never compare the job's title instead: once the Change path refreshes the stored item, the title still differs, and the check would fire at every claim. The pre-claim check below makes the same comparison, and there a fast-path job is one whose title equals `<work name>: <stored item>` for its single key.

<!-- atom id=draft.input-check ops=input_check -->
**The input check.** It runs before the assigning write for any Ready job whose id is in session entry's `opened.ingest_inputs.ready_jobs`, or, after a later render, in the Job Board document's `ingest_inputs.ready_jobs`. A job not in that list, a proceeding-wide job or a job on a Writing whose type declares no inputs, has no input check.

- Ask `/case-data` for the ingest coverage of exactly the folders in `ingest_inputs.folders`, the solicitor-client and correspondence folders among them counting as correspondence. Never read a `/case-data` file or the database for it.
- Clean, with nothing to ingest, nothing damaged or unreadable and no folder missing: one line naming the folders checked, then go on to assign.
- Otherwise assign nothing. Say `inputs_stale: <folder> (<N> files)` for each folder with files to ingest, and name any damaged or unreadable file and any missing folder. Word each file to ingest by its entry in the coverage's `records`, and make one offer for it:
  - `possible_version_of` holds an id N, whatever the verdict: say the file may be another version of source #N, which case data already holds, and offer `/case-data`'s `supersede-draft` on that pair. Never offer `/ingest` on it. Send both files' cards in the same turn (`/show-me`), asking `/case-data` for the other file's path. `/draft` writes no case data: the supersession is `/case-data`'s.
  - verdict `partial` and no `possible_version_of`: say the file is partly ingested, and offer `/ingest` on it.
  - verdict `failed` and no `possible_version_of`: say an ingest of the file failed, and offer `/ingest` on it.
  - verdict `none` and no `possible_version_of`: the file has no record. Offer `/ingest` on it.
- On a yes, run what was offered, check again, then assign. A file still not held after that keeps the job unassigned. On a no, assign nothing and wait: the job stays in Ready. A `/case-data` error is quoted in one line and nothing is assigned: never say the folders are ingested when the check could not run.
- In an unattended run (`references/dispatch.md` section 14), anything but a clean result goes in the run's report and the run stops.

<!-- atom id=draft.pre-claim ops=pre_claim -->
**The pre-claim check.** For a ready one-step job the premise check runs before the assigning write, not after it, because once assigned a job whose work has gone is this session's own, which it may not close, and letting go of it would end the session's job work for nothing. It reads the job's card, `board_card(jobs: [N])`, and its `keys:` line. A title-restating step (below) or practitioner-only work fails it: assign nothing, say which, wait. A job whose every key is dead takes the "Every key dead" row first, whatever its step says, because its work has gone.

| Result | What the session does |
|---|---|
| Every key derives and no item changed, or the job carries no keys | One line, then assign and dispatch. Still no pause. |
| Every key dead | Assign nothing. Say the marker no longer fires, then one decision form: close it as superseded, or keep it and say what it should now do. |
| Some keys dead (a hand-merged one-step job) | Assign nothing. The same form without the close option. |
| Item changed on a fast-path job, meaning one whose title is its Proposal's own `<work name>: <old item>` | No silent dispatch. Assign nothing, show both texts, and offer three choices: run it as worded, close it as superseded, or re-outline it. |
| Item changed on a hand-worded one-step job | Report only, then assign and dispatch. |

<!-- atom id=draft.pre-claim.outcomes ops=pre_claim -->
Closing as superseded is bookkeeping by `/drafting-data` §9b, then a re-render. Keeping it or re-outlining it is an outline afresh of that job (Branch B, "A Blocked job whose blocker is superseded or shelved"), whose write drops the dead keys, and the session then assigns nothing. Running it as worded assigns and dispatches the recorded intent unchanged. A taken-over job gets the read-only report in `references/take-over.md` instead.

<!-- atom id=draft.pre-claim.unattended ops=pre_claim when=unattended:true -->
In an unattended run (`references/dispatch.md` section 14) nothing is shown and nothing waits. The first row, and the last after its report line, assign and dispatch. Every other row assigns nothing: the run reports the result, both texts where an item changed, and stops.

<!-- atom id=draft.premise.never-holds ops=premise_check,pre_claim -->
**Never say the premise holds.** Say each key's marker still fires, which is the whole of what the check knows: it inherits every marker defect the renderer has. Never report a shift of glyph within a key that still derives, because the conversion record stores no marker snapshot and which glyph stood at outlining is unknowable.

<!-- atom id=draft.premise.failed-path ops=plan_readback,change when=premise:failed -->
**The failed path is a scoped Change, never a refusal.** A grouped intake job with some keys still live takes its own rule instead (below). Name the moved items, ask one question in the outlining interview's manner ("with these out, how should the steps read?"), record it verbatim, read it back once, and write it per `/drafting-data` §5. Where the session proposes the wording from the record instead, it takes the adoption ceremony (`shared/paralegal-posture.md` section 3). One turn whether one key moved or twenty. That same write clears the dead keys from the job's conversion record: the practitioner has just dropped that work, and a marker firing on that row again must not stay hidden behind this job. The check never refuses the job, never releases the lock, and never sends the job to a fresh outline. It is one of the two stops before execution (Announce and proceed, below), never a second pause. A one-step job is checked before it is assigned instead, by the pre-claim check above.

<!-- atom id=draft.steps ops=plan_readback when=job_steps:many -->
**Steps:** the steps written with the job at outlining are its plan, in the practitioner's recorded words. They are never re-divided or reworded at assignment: a replaced step list needs the practitioner's own words (`/drafting-data` §5), so a re-division would be a pause. Where the practitioner asks for different steps, that is the Change path below. There, one action, or one action repeated over a set the approved wording names ("pull and read three follow-up cases"), is one step, and actions that differ in kind or depend on each other's result ("audit the three memos; record the verified propositions with pinpoints; re-render") are separate steps. A division the session proposes is drawn from the record and takes the adoption ceremony (`shared/paralegal-posture.md` section 3).

<!-- atom id=draft.steps.list ops=change,new_work -->
**Steps given as a list.** When the practitioner gives steps as a numbered or bulleted list, each item is one step, its number or bullet removed and its words unchanged. The practitioner-words gate accepts this because each item is an unbroken run of that turn.

<!-- atom id=draft.steps.restated ops=plan_readback,take_over -->
**A step that only restates the title.** Before any announcement of steps in this Branch, compare each step not yet done with the job's title, ignoring case, surrounding and repeated whitespace, and trailing punctuation. A step that matches names no work: the title says what the job is about, not what to do. Never announce it as a step to run, and never ask whether it is still right. In your visible reply say that step N only restates the job's title and so names no work, then ask how it should read. Propose no wording. While any such step stands, the job does not proceed: the answer is the Change path below, recorded in the practitioner's words, read back once, and written by `/drafting-data` §5. On a taken-over or restored job, say before asking that the inherited stamps will be discarded. A step already done is shown as done and is not checked. A newly assigned one-step job is dispatched on its recorded intent as below, unchanged.

<!-- atom id=draft.one-step ops=pre_claim,take_over -->
**A newly assigned one-step job** runs on its creation approval. No pause: the pre-claim check above has already run. Its one step, written at creation, is the plan. It takes the header below (Announce and proceed), 0 of 1 steps done, then dispatches. An intake job's creation is the intake itself (`references/dispatch.md` section 14).

<!-- atom id=draft.note-up ops=note_up -->
**An intake note-up job on a statute, regulation or rule** keeps the steps intake wrote, one per provision the Writing cites (`/drafting-data` §3e). Its steps are its plan, never re-divided or replaced by the recorded intent: a single step runs as the one-step job above with that step unchanged, and two or more are announced as written. One written with no step asks the practitioner for its steps before it proceeds and proposes none. The job runs the currency check through `/legal-research` once, at its first step, naming every step's provision, and the gate relays the enactment's one legislation record (`shared/legal-research.md`, "Statute currency."). Every later step reuses that record and checks nothing again, and so does any other job on the same enactment in this session (the record's `key`). Intake writes the note-ups of one Act as one job, `Note up: <Act>`, holding every key that names the Act and one step per provision (`/drafting-data` §3e, "One title, one job"), so that one check serves them all. Such a job records the record's `path` and `currency` on every authority its keys name, not only the first. Each step quotes its provision by pinpoint from the file at the record's `path`, never from a summary, a memo or a CanLII call. It records through `/drafting-data` the record's `path` as the authority's locator, its `currency` as the authority's currency result, and its `currency_marker` as that provision's "current to" date. A record whose `path` is `none` leaves the provision unquoted, and the step says so. A grouped intake job (`/drafting-data` §3e), one step per Proposal holding its item, keeps its steps the same way: they are its plan, never re-divided, and are announced as written. On the premise check a dead key drops that key's step and keeps the others as written. That is a Change under `/drafting-data` §5 with no adoption ceremony and no wording question, because every step that stays is record text the practitioner-words gate passes. When every key is dead, no step would stay, so it takes the failed path above unchanged.

<!-- atom id=draft.announce ops=pre_claim,plan_readback,take_over -->
**Announce and proceed.** Every job this session works that is not completed, newly assigned, taken over or restored, one step or many, is announced and then worked without asking. This is the standing rule in place of a plan approval. Once the premise check and the title-restating check above have passed, print this header in your own visible reply, never only inside widget markup or text between tool calls, which some runtimes summarize away:

1. **Which job:** `Job <N>`, by number, never the `# Job <N>: <title>` heading again, which is printed once, when the job is assigned (SKILL.md, Job header); then the recorded intent quoted verbatim, adding "outlined in an earlier session, not re-checked since" when it was outlined in an earlier session, and the premise check's one line.
2. **Progress so far:** `<d> of <m> steps done`, listing each step done in an earlier session in its recorded words, or "none yet". Where the job's card prints open sources to get, list them on a line of their own, `Sources still to get: <labels>`, apart from the steps: they are the job's record, never part of the plan.
3. **Planned this session:** each step not yet done, in its recorded words, as legal work ("audit the three memos; record the verified propositions with pinpoints; re-render"), never as tool calls or database operations, with no substance added.
4. **Order:** those steps as a numbered list in the order they will run.

Then print exactly `Proceeding with the planned work now.` and, before the first step runs, stamp the approval with `drafting_data__approve_plan` (`/drafting-data` §5). On an inherited plan it returns `already_approved` and writes nothing, so the inherited stamps survive. Then execute end to end, stamping each step as it completes, until the job is complete. An unattended run prints no header and dispatches as `references/unattended-run.md` says.

The only stops before the first step are a dead key (the failed path above) and a step that only restates the title (above). A reworded item is reported in the header and does not stop. Once work runs, the only stops are:

- work the session cannot do itself: an obstacle the plan did not anticipate (An obstacle on any assigned job, below), or human-only work, which is listed for the practitioner (Movers, below);
- a one-way-door act: recording a Writing filed or sent, retirement, taking over or superseding another session's job, a deletion, or waiving a pre-filing check;
- a hook refusal, quoted verbatim, then stop (SKILL.md, the no-blocking-checkpoint rule);
- anything deadline isolation forbids (SKILL.md, deadline isolation);
- a statement of law, which goes only through `/legal-research`.

A two-way-door choice, one a later write can undo, proceeds without asking.

<!-- atom id=draft.readback.change ops=change -->
- **Change**, when the practitioner asks for one or a stop above requires it: one question at a time, in the outlining interview's shape (below), recorded in their words, read back once, approved, executed. Same job, same session. Nothing new is assigned and nothing new is outlined. A re-approval replaces the full step list and re-stamps the approval. A change the practitioner words themselves needs no ceremony. A change the session proposes takes the adoption ceremony (`shared/paralegal-posture.md` section 3).

<!-- atom id=draft.obstacle ops=obstacle -->
**An obstacle on any assigned job**, one-step, taken-over or restored included, is a permitted interruption once its work runs (Announce and proceed, above, lists the others): something the plan did not anticipate. First read the matter folder and case data on it. Ask the practitioner only what those leave open. State it in one line of prose in your reply, never a list or step. Then offer only change, a scope expansion (letting the job edit more) if the work needs material outside its scope, or spin off blocker jobs if the obstacle is separate work this job must wait on, such as a research question or a document to obtain. Never abandon or defer. Say only what each does to the job: no example, fact, figure or blocker title. Content follows the choice (`shared/paralegal-posture.md` section 3). In an unattended run nothing is offered: it reports the obstacle and stops (`references/unattended-run.md`).

<!-- atom id=draft.obstacle.change ops=obstacle,change when=obstacle:in_job -->
- **Change:** the Change path above. On a one-step job it replaces the full step list and re-stamps the approval, as for any job (`/drafting-data` §5). As there, a change the practitioner words themselves needs no ceremony, and one the session proposes takes it. On a taken-over or restored job, say first that the inherited stamps will be discarded. Where the change hands a step to the practitioner, the re-approved step is in their words and lists as theirs under Movers (below). The job stays open and its marker keeps firing.

<!-- atom id=draft.execute ops=execute -->
### Executing the plan as a worklist

The markers on the mockup are the worklist. There is no second task list to build or drift out of sync with the document.

<!-- atom id=draft.execute.threads ops=execute -->
- **Loose threads before research:** before researching anything for the job, check the `## Loose Threads` lines of `board_card(lanes: ["loose_threads"])` for ideas on the job's topic. They are every open loose thread in the proceeding, and one captured on a sibling Writing names that Writing in its closing parenthesis (`from Job #N on <Writing title>`, or `from <Writing title>`). Reading a loose thread does not consume it: only a later outlining session does, or the job whose intent is working the loose threads, which consumes the thread it worked.
<!-- atom id=draft.execute.loose-threads-job ops=execute,plan_readback,pre_claim,route_reply when=job_held:true -->
- **The loose-threads job:** when the held job's recorded intent is working the loose threads, read `references/loose-thread-triage.md`, "Working the threads without a pick", and follow it. The threads are the job's work: it asks no pick question, and an instruction about how it does that work, a bare "continue" included, is applied and never captured as a loose thread.
<!-- atom id=draft.execute.lock ops=execute -->
- **Lock check before each research dispatch:** immediately before every `research-paralegal` dispatch and every law-audit agent spawn the job makes, through `/legal-research` or otherwise, run `/drafting-data`'s lock read before a dispatch on the job. Only this session's own token on it dispatches. Anything else dispatches nothing: stop and report what the row shows, naming any answer already saved under the job. A job taken over in another session is no longer this session's to research, and no write guard stops a dispatch.
<!-- atom id=draft.execute.sources ops=execute -->
- **Sources to get:** before a dispatch that retrieves a decision or an enactment, and before extracting or ingesting a file, record each one on the job with `drafting_data__track_source`, and close it once it has landed (`/drafting-data`'s `jobs-common.md` §2, "Sources to get"). That call is the lock check for the dispatch. A source is the job's own record and never a step: the plan and its step count stay as approved, and no Change is taken to add one. Its label is a citation, an instrument or a file name, never what an authority holds and never a deadline.
<!-- atom id=draft.execute.save ops=execute -->
- **Saving an answer under the job:** save each research answer the job receives with `research_filing__save_answer`, passing the job's `db_path`, `ticket_id` and this session's `session_token`, so the job's card lists it and its idle clock restarts. A result reading `job.recorded: false` means the job is no longer this session's: the file is saved, so report it and stop.
<!-- atom id=draft.execute.flags ops=execute -->
- **Case-data flags in a research return:** read each `case-data:` line in the return's notes (`shared/legal-audit-protocol.md` Step 6), in the same turn the return arrives. Capture comes first (SKILL.md section 14, "Captured ideas"): every `pinpoint` flag together as one idea, and each other flag as its own. A `pinpoint` flag ends there, and the job carries on. Each `discrepancy` or `correction` flag is separate work this job must wait on: say so in one line and take the Spin off path above without being asked, proposing one blocker per flag. Its two values are fixed frames, `<kind>` being the flag's kind and `<flag>` its description and evidence ids, character for character: title `Resolve the case-data <kind>: <flag>`, intent `What should case data record? <flag>`. Put both in the visible reply as plain text, each whole on one line, and say they come from the research return. The rest is the Spin off path unchanged. Whichever session is later assigned the blocker records the answer through `/case-data`, and the session states no law. A return with no `case-data:` line changes nothing.

<!-- atom id=draft.execute.markers ops=execute -->
- **Reading markers:** a clean line means the component is done, nothing prints for done. Our own outstanding work renders after the component it belongs to; events caused by the other side or the world render as a prefix in front of the work they routed. Applying "what work is left" to an event marker is a category error.
<!-- atom id=draft.execute.movers ops=execute -->
- **Movers:** every marker maps to a mover. Retrievable work goes to subagents. Law goes through `references/authorities.md`. Evidence, witness statements and admissions go through `references/evidence.md`. Human-only work (a deponent swearing, a witness signing) is listed for the practitioner and never dispatched or silently dropped. A statement whose scrutiny failed has its review job as its next step, and that job's decision is the practitioner's, never a subagent's, and the statement is never listed as needing fresh scrutiny (`references/authorities.md` Failed scrutiny). The `Prepare the review pages` job runs by `references/counsel-review.md`. The `Link or draft the factual basis` job first asks whether the test is on file: `/drafting-data`'s read of the submission's legal basis (its scrutiny reference, "Read a submission's legal basis"), on the submission the job's key names, run as the job's first act and before any fact is shown. The test is on file when that read shows at least one statement verified or settled. When it does not, say so in one line as the obstacle, then take the Spin off path above without being asked, proposing one research blocker: title `Research the test: <contention>`, intent `What must be shown to establish this contention? <contention>`, `<contention>` being the item on the job's `keys:` line, character for character. The two frames are fixed and the contention is the record's own words, so the session states no law, and which rules or tests apply is `/legal-research`'s to answer, never split out here. Put both values in the visible reply as plain text, each whole on one line, say they come from the job's Proposal item, and the rest is the Spin off path unchanged. With the test on file, the job opens with a proposed set of facts already on file to link to the contention, each with its source, a one-line reason it bears on the contention, and any fact on file that cuts against it, and asks the practitioner to approve or change the set (`shared/paralegal-posture.md` section 3, propose from the record). No link is written before their yes. Where no fact on file supports the contention, the job says so as a gap and never fills it with a fact the record does not hold. It closes when facts are linked, or when counsel states in their own words why the contention needs no stated fact (recorded by `/drafting-data`), a reason the record cannot supply, which the skill asks for and never proposes. The `Link or draft the proposition it requires` job is its law-side mirror: it proposes a proposition already on file to link, with its one-line reason, and writes no link before the practitioner's yes; with none on file it names the obstacle in one line and spins off the same `Research the test: <contention>` blocker with the same two frames; it closes when a proposition is linked, or when counsel states in their own words why the contention needs no stated law (recorded by `/drafting-data`), which the skill asks for and never proposes; and while it stands on the same submission, the factual-basis job's test-on-file step names the proposition job as the obstacle and spins off nothing, so one piece of law work is never routed twice. A letter is drafted as a Writing, saved first (`references/procedure.md` section 8) with its stock jobs, by `references/letters.md`. Open days the letter offers as hearing dates come from `/practice-data` court-calendar-days, fetched by this session. The `Availability letter` job's letter is BCSC's `letter` kind, saved on the proceeding of the job's own Writing, and its sending is the Sent write of the `Deliver` job that save writes. A stock `Availability letter` job's plan is always the one step `/drafting-data` §3b gives now, whatever intent and steps its record carries.
<!-- atom id=draft.execute.rhythm ops=execute -->
- **One at a time:** the practitioner says what is done about each marker, one at a time, in their own words. The list that runs is theirs, read back.
- **Fan-out:** how the list fans out is a mechanical choice stated in one line and made by the skill. Independent items run in parallel, items touching the same passage run in turn, an item waiting on another runs once that answer lands.
- **Reporting:** short summaries, interrupt only when stuck.
- **Regeneration:** one regeneration at the end of a dispatched batch, revision bumped, presented as a file card. Markers whose work finished are gone; human-only markers remain until the practitioner clears them.

<!-- atom id=draft.opposing ops=opposing_points -->
### Opposing points

Where a point attacks is asked, never inferred, offered as the Writing's own fixed list of submissions and orders. An opposing point is captured the moment it is forwarded, whatever the session was about. Where an answer leaves part of a point unmet, ask about that part, never compose the rest (facts the matter already holds may be offered for the practitioner to pick from). Deliberate silence is recorded, not implied: a point held for the hearing, or declined outright, is a decision on the record. The finish line is zero unrecorded rebuttals, not zero unanswered points.

### Complete

Before completing a job converted from Proposals, re-render and check which of its keys still derive on its card (`board_card(jobs: [N])`), by `/drafting-data` §9: while any does, name it in one line ("its marker still fires on N of M items") and complete only on the practitioner's yes, otherwise the job stays in progress; an unattended run reports and stops. A job with a source to get still open is completed the same way: named in one line, and only on the practitioner's yes (`/drafting-data`'s `jobs-common.md` §2, "Before completing a job with open sources"). Completion is the `drafting_data__complete_job` call, which also rewrites the Job Board, so no separate render follows it. After it succeeds, add the job's completed entry to `ProceduralHistory.md` (`shared/procedural-history.md`). When done, the session is assigned no other job. An unfinished job stays in progress under this session's lock and surfaces for take-over next session, unless the practitioner tells the session to release or shelve it (`references/shelve-and-restore.md`), after which the session is assigned no other job either.

## Branch B: new work (outlining)

Records jobs, dependencies, blockers, conflicts, priorities. Never substance: no proceeding or Writing row either (section 7). The practitioner supplies everything the record cannot; the session asks, records and reads back. Where the record holds the candidates, it proposes them per `shared/paralegal-posture.md` section 3. It proposes nothing the record cannot supply and never fills a silence.

<!-- atom id=draft.outline.start ops=new_work -->
- **An empty board:** say so and ask what work the application needs. Ask who to interview, what to locate, what to ask the client, and their theory of the case. Offer no candidates. Where the board carries Proposals but no job and no loose thread, the routing here is unchanged but the wording is not: say the board has no jobs yet, never that it is empty, because it still carries Proposals. On a Job Board with no Writing, ask what work the board is for in place of what the application needs, and write each job to that board, never proceeding-wide.
- Markers, and any problem the practitioner reports, may be read into and shown. What to do is proposed per the posture only where the record holds the candidates, and a gap the record cannot fill is asked empty-handed. A fix for a problem the practitioner reports is never proposed (SKILL.md section 2). The mockup already shows those same markers, so re-reading them into chat adds nothing the practitioner is not looking at.
- Ask what they have in mind, and raise each captured idea (`board_card(lanes: ["loose_threads"])`).
- After that opening question, before any read-back, run the currency check of `shared/procedural-history.md` on the matter's `ProceduralHistory.md`. Where the outlining needs the history, read the whole file. Never ask the practitioner to recount what happened: the check's one question is the only question about the history. A past event read from the file is content. Never look a date up through deadline work.

<!-- atom id=draft.outline.pick ops=new_work -->
**A Proposal picked.** The Proposed lane is never pointed at as a starting menu: no Proposal is offered, named first or ranked, and the case-context questions above come first whatever that lane shows, except for an unedited pick named at the Opening, which is written first and never raised at the closing read-back. A job intake wrote in this session (`references/dispatch.md` section 14) is already on the board and is not raised there either. A pick is received whenever the practitioner makes one, and read off `board_card(lanes: ["proposed"])`. On the first, say in one sentence that each Proposal picked unedited becomes its own one-step job, and that naming several in words as one piece of work merges them instead. What follows turns on whether they changed its fixed label.

<!-- atom id=draft.outline.pick-paths ops=new_work -->
- **Left unedited:** the pick is the approval. Write a one-step job whose title and intent are the Proposal's own fixed text, `<work name>: <item>`, character for character, and whose single step is its key type's fixed step frame on that item (`/drafting-data` §3, the fast path), so that the step names the work rather than restating the title, and the pre-claim check passes it. No read-back, no approve step, no question of any kind. Several unedited picks are several such jobs, one each: the session never folds picks together, the interview's "how the work divides into jobs and steps is the session's own call" is no licence to.
- **Its label changed:** the fast path is off, because nothing fixed is left to fill the intent and the step. Their edited words are an answer like any other, and the job is outlined through the interview below and confirmed at the closing read-back. The title is cut from the edited text. The intent is one sentence of theirs, the edited text itself where that is a sentence, otherwise asked. Never compose one out of the edited words and the Proposal's text.
- **Several named as one piece of work:** a merge, and only on their own words saying so. It is outlined through the interview: its title is an unbroken run of their words from the answer naming the work, never one of the Proposals' labels, and where that answer has nothing to cut from it is a gap put as a question, as at the closing read-back. The merged job records every key it was outlined from.
- **A picked key already held or shelved:** read each picked key's lane off `board_card(key: <key>)` when the pick is received: a key the card returns as a live job, or as a Proposed line reading `lane: held by #N …`, is held; a key it returns under Shelved is shelved. A held or shelved key gets no job and no conversion record, however it was picked, and its work is not outlined here. Name each holder in one line, and each shelved key as shelved and restorable on request. A shelved key is never restored implicitly: restoring is the practitioner's own request (`references/dispatch.md` section 14, "Shelve and restore"), and a key restored after its pick was refused is picked afresh. Picked keys still in Proposed carry on down the path their pick took, and a merge that lost a key asks afresh what the remaining work is called and what it is. Nothing is taken over or assigned, and the interview runs until the practitioner ends it. A holder they ask for is named at the Opening, restated after the closing read-back, so the answers gathered so far are recorded first.

<!-- atom id=draft.outline.pick-record ops=new_work -->
A job outlined from a Proposal, on any of those three paths, carries the conversion record and takes its Writing, scope and billing anchor by `/drafting-data` (`/drafting-data` §3).

<!-- atom id=draft.outline.shelved ops=new_work,shelve -->
**A Proposal shelved** in the interview is applied when they say it, as the bookkeeping `/drafting-data` specifies, and is not written as a job. It is neither outlining nor a tangent. Shelving is received, never offered: no Proposal is put up for it, and the three-way choice offered for a captured idea is not offered here.

<!-- atom id=draft.outline.interview ops=new_work,change -->
**The outlining interview** (Branch B, and Branch A's change path): one question at a time, answers recorded in their words, closing read-back confirmed before any write. Only the practitioner ends it, by saying there is nothing further, never infer the end. How the work divides into jobs and steps, and what waits on what, is the session's own call and is never asked.

- The application: what is sought, against whom, on what basis, what evidence is in hand, what the other side will say, what must be in place before it is heard. Where scope is open, confirm it back in their terms.
- Which Writing or proceeding the work belongs to is data: the open board's, unless another Writing, forum or proceeding is named, then asked; a new one goes to section 8.
- Gaps as questions: where a figure comes from, what the court will be told, what evidence exists. Never the content.
- Order: only "which of these cannot both happen?" Never ask which job comes first, and never assert a priority. Jobs that depend on each other take their order from the dependency, and the rest are worked in parallel. An order the practitioner volunteers is recorded.
- Legal views: record as their proposition and ask whether they want it verified as a research job before anything depends on it. Ask that of each proposition before the read-back. No structure or order decision stands in for it. Never dispatch unasked, never treat it as settled, never write the session's own legal impression.
- Tension: name both, ask which stands.
- Captured ideas: quote each exactly as `board_card(lanes: ["loose_threads"])` prints it, but with internal ids and terms put in a lawyer's words (if unable, say so and ask what it refers to), and offer a three-way choice, outline it now, leave it, or drop it, with no recommendation among the three. Outlining consumes the idea, leaving touches nothing, dropping discards it (a struck line that can be restored). With a large backlog the practitioner may take the triage route instead (`references/loose-thread-triage.md`), which ranks every open thread on the Loose Threads page; inside it the practitioner's word still decides whether each thread is outlined, left or dropped. That file's working route ("Working the threads without a pick") takes a thread's work into the held job without a per-thread choice, and runs only on the instruction to work on the loose threads or in the job whose intent is to work them.

<!-- atom id=draft.blocked-job ops=new_work -->
**A Blocked job whose blocker is superseded or shelved** is raised beside loose threads. State from the record what it was waiting on and what happened to that — a blocker superseded by a retirement has no superseding job, so what happened is the Writing's retirement. Then ask open, "How do you want to handle it?", never suggesting a new figure and never proposing what the fresh outline should say. Leaving it writes nothing. Read back the job's existing steps, off its card (`board_card(jobs: [N])`), to confirm or change rather than re-planning them, and give the same key report Branch A's premise check gives, read-only: marker state is the same kind of record fact as what the job was waiting on. Only the report carries over, never Branch A's failed path — a fresh outline is not an assigned job's Change, it leaves the conversion record alone, and what the practitioner does about a moved key is one more thing they say in the interview.

<!-- atom id=draft.blocked-job.rules ops=new_work -->
- **It is outlining, and it happens in place.** The session is never assigned the job, and never outlines one afresh inside an assigned job's session, which has the Change path instead. The only creations an assigned job's session makes are the spin-off of blocker jobs (Branch A, "An obstacle on any assigned job") and the fixed-text creates (`/drafting-data` §3g, §3i). Such a session outlines and consumes no loose thread, with one exception: a job whose intent is working the loose threads works and consumes the threads it takes (`references/loose-thread-triage.md`, "Working the threads without a pick"), and still creates no job for them. Every other assigned job is bound as before. What may change, what never changes, and the write itself are `/drafting-data` §3a's.
- **Confirming the step list unaltered keeps the stamps on steps already done.** Any rewording, reordering, addition or removal replaces the steps and clears the approval with them. Say which of the two is happening before they choose, not after.
- **It cannot keep a stale waiting-on**, or it lands straight back in the same Job list. Ask what it waits on now, if anything. They may drop it, name something else, or restore a blocker they shelved while it was queued. A newly named blocker may not itself be superseded or shelved.

<!-- atom id=draft.outline.closing ops=new_work -->
**Closing read-back.** Proposals picked unedited are already written, one job each, and get no card: never present their titles again for a second selection, which could only be answered yes. Nothing is asked about them. A research offer job (`/drafting-data` §3i) is already written too, and gets no card. Put the other jobs back in their words, in dependency order and otherwise the order they were outlined in, as one multi-select widget: a card per job with its title, a one-line intent, a subtitle carrying any waiting-on or either-or relationship, card value is the title, written unchanged, never re-derived. Two more subtitles come off the job's origin: "from Proposals" for one outlined from Proposals, and "changes to Job #N" for one outlined afresh. Where a job has both an origin and a relationship to carry, the origin goes first. Title, intent and the steps written with the job are cut from the recorded answers, never rewritten. The intent is one sentence of theirs, verbatim (a stock bundle job's is `/drafting-data` §3b's fixed text), a question kept as a question (a one-step job later runs on that string, Plan above). The title is an unbroken run of their own words from one answer, no word swapped or moved, taking in their verb for the work where they used one. Where the instruction and its subject sit in different turns ("research it"), the subject's turn supplies the words. A job with nothing to cut from is a gap: put it as a question, never as a card. A stock bundle job is carded only under `/prepare`, never as a gap: its card carries §3b's fixed title. Under `/draft`, work one covers is outlined in their words, never §3b's. Nothing preselected, submit only. The selection is the confirmation, no separate approve step. One job is not a list: with exactly one card, use the ordinary approve-or-change form instead, except that a lone stock bundle job is picked or declined, never changed. Write the selected jobs in dependency order, blocker before blocked, and drop any dependency on an unselected job, saying so. Say beforehand, in one sentence, what happens to anything left unselected: an unselected job derived from a captured idea stays a captured idea; one outlined afresh stays exactly as it was, in its Job list; one outlined from Proposals is not written, and its Proposals stay in Proposed; any other unselected job is gone at session end. Selecting nothing is valid and writes nothing. A chat reply naming jobs is a valid submission. Close by rendering, which updates the Writing's Job Board page, never with a chat summary.
