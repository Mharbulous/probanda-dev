# VanIAC domestic arbitration, Part B Expedited Procedures

Rule text lives in `Sources/`. Quote a rule only as it stands there. "R." means the VanIAC Domestic Arbitration Rules; "Part A" marks a Part A rule relied on to fill a Part B silence.

**How Part A fills gaps.** Part B governs. A Part A rule may be relied on only to fill a gap Part B leaves, and only where it contradicts no Part B provision. Name the gap each time a Part A rule is used.

## 1. Commencement

- Part B assumes an arbitration has already begun but never says when it begins (R. 24(d) has the Centre advise of an expedited arbitration when it advises the commencement date). Part A, R. 6(a) fills that gap: the arbitration is deemed commenced when the Notice to Arbitrate has been delivered to the Centre and the commencement fee paid, and the Centre notifies the parties of the deemed commencement date.
- Evidence of commencement: a proceeding record showing the arbitration commenced, with its date, or the Centre's R. 6(a) commencement notice.
- The exchange package exists only inside a commenced arbitration. A claim advanced in the package must already have been raised by a delivered Notice to Arbitrate or Notice of Counterclaim; a claim missing its notice is flagged to the practitioner, never cured by drafting the notice.

## 2. Whether Part B applies, and the award cap

- **R. 24(a).** Part B applies if (i) the parties agree, or (ii) no claim, or estimate of the amount claimed, by any one party in a Notice to Arbitrate or Notice of Counterclaim exceeds $250,000 exclusive of interest and costs. Either limb suffices.
- **R. 24(c).** Part B does not apply if the parties agreed to a tribunal of more than one arbitrator, agreed to opt out, or the Centre determined before appointment that the arbitration is unsuitable. Only an affirmative ground in the record counts; silence about an opt-out is not a ground.
- **R. 24(h).** A new or amended claim or counterclaim over $250,000, exclusive of interest and costs, needs the tribunal's consent.
- **R. 24(j).** The tribunal cannot award a total over $250,000 exclusive of interest and costs to any one party, unless it consented under R. 24(h).
- Test the amounts the notices state, as recorded; do not recompute them from the evidence. A stated amount at or under $250,000 complies however it is composed. An amount over $250,000 where the record does not say whether interest or costs are included: surface R. 24(j) and ask for the exclusive figure.
- Surface the R. 24(j) cap before anything is drafted whenever any amount exceeds it, naming the claim, the party, the amount and whether an R. 24(h) consent is recorded. It is a warning, never a refusal: the practitioner decides whether to proceed.

## 3. Parties and labels

- Part B never assigns party labels. Part A, R. 5(a) fills that gap: a claimant commences by delivering a Notice to Arbitrate to the respondent(s) and the Centre.

  | The client | Style |
  |---|---|
  | delivered the Notice to Arbitrate that commenced the arbitration | **Claimant** |
  | received that Notice to Arbitrate | **Respondent** |

- Several respondents: keep the numbering the exchanged documents already use, otherwise number them in the order the Notice to Arbitrate names them.
- **A counterclaim does not change the label.** A respondent that delivered a Notice of Counterclaim (R. 24(e)) remains the Respondent; style its claim "the Respondent's counterclaim". A claimant answering a counterclaim remains the Claimant.
- **Forbidden labels:** court labels, for either side: "Plaintiff", "Defendant", "Petitioner", "Claimant by counterclaim", "Counterclaim Claimant" and variants.
- The decision-maker is **the Arbitral Tribunal**.
