## BCCA Reckoning

These rules apply to every deadline in this file. The Interpretation Act applies to both the Rules and the Act (s. 2(1) "every enactment"; the Rules are a "regulation" and hence an "enactment", s. 1) except where a contrary intention appears — Rule 2 is one such contrary intention (see the landing-day policy).

**Business day** (R. 1(1)): "a day on which the court registries are open to the public." R. 83(1): registries are open 9 a.m.–4 p.m. "on each day other than a Saturday or a holiday", unless the registrar directs otherwise (public notice required, R. 83(2)). "Holiday" (Interpretation Act s. 29) **includes Sunday**, Christmas Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Canada Day, Victoria Day, British Columbia Day, Labour Day, National Day for Truth and Reconciliation, Remembrance Day, Family Day, New Year's Day, December 26, and days set or proclaimed as public holidays. **Thanksgiving (the second Monday of October) is an open question, not a settled holiday or business day.** It is not named in paras. (a)–(c) of s. 29 "holiday". It is a holiday only if para. (d) captures it, as a day "appointed by proclamation of the Governor General or the Lieutenant Governor, to be observed as a day of ... public rejoicing or thanksgiving", through a standing proclamation. That proclamation (cited in a Westlaw memo as SOR/57-56) has not been read. Separately, a registrar's direction on public notice can close the registries (R. 83(1)–(2)), which makes the day a non-business day under R. 1(1) whatever s. 29 says, and no such notice is on file. Source: firm research memo, 2026-10-15, "ML re Thanksgiving holiday Court of Appeal business day"; Interpretation Act s. 29 "holiday" paras. (a)–(d); Court of Appeal Rules R. 1(1), 83(1)–(2). **Until it is settled, compute both treatments and store the cautious date for the row's direction: the earlier date for a deadline to act, the later date for an earliest-permissible floor.** In practice: a forward deadline to act, or one whose count lands on Thanksgiving and shifts forward, treats Thanksgiving as a business day; an earliest-permissible floor treats it as a holiday. **A backward business-day count that crosses Thanksgiving is the exception**: treating it as a holiday skips it and gives the earlier acting day, so that is the cautious date there, as it is for a backward plain-days row that lands on it (the landing-day policy then stores the earlier day). Flag every row whose date differs between the two treatments, and never call a filing late, or a hearing date too early, on this one-day margin. Any of three sources would settle the point: the 1957 proclamation read against para. (d), the registrar's published notices of registry hours and closures, and the Court of Appeal Act, SBC 2021, c 6, for any definition of "holiday". Saturday is **not** a s. 29 holiday — R. 83(1) names it separately. Net: **a business day is any day that is not a Saturday, not a s. 29 holiday, and not a registrar-directed closure day.** The Interpretation Act does not make an observed substitute day (e.g. a Monday observance of a weekend holiday) itself a holiday — for edge years, confirm against the registry's published closures rather than asserting.

**Counting "at least N business days before" a reference day** (every chambers deadline below): Interpretation Act s. 25.2(2)(b) — the reference day is after the period, so count backward "from and including the day before the reference day" — plus s. 25.2(3)(b) — a period expressed as "at least"/"not less than"/"clear" ends **one day earlier**. The Rules supply the unit (business days, R. 1(1)); the Act supplies the mechanics. Net effect: **N full business days must lie clear between the acting day and the reference day, neither endpoint counted.** ("Clear days" appears nowhere in these Rules — "at least" does that work via s. 25.2(3).) **That s. 25.2 reaches a period whose unit is the business day is an assumption, not a verified construction — see [reckoning.md](reckoning.md) § Clear-day / exclusive counting, "Assumption, not verified construction (#740)". #740 put it to the research gate and no authority was located either way. If s. 25.2 does not reach such a period, every date this algorithm produces is one business day later than shown — the cautious direction for the filing party, but never a basis for calling a filing late by one day.** **#746 exhausted the remaining research routes and the assumption is now confirmed as permanent, not merely open: all three of #740's unread leads plus the two authorities they rest on were retrieved and read, and the gate reports no authority deciding the point either way. Registry practice on CA59991 independently enforced the date this algorithm gives (Package 4377698, 02-SEP-2026, "the 2 clear business days deadline"). See [reckoning.md](reckoning.md) § Clear-day / exclusive counting, "Confirmed as a permanent assumption (#746)".**

**Backward algorithm (at least N business days before):** (1) start at the day before the reference day (s. 25.2(2)(b)); (2) step backward counting only business days until N are counted (that starting day counts as business day 1 if it is one); (3) take one further calendar day earlier (s. 25.2(3)(b)); (4) if that final day is not a business day, apply the landing-day policy below. Worked check: hearing Friday 2026-09-04, N=2 → Thu 09-03 (1), Wed 09-02 (2), one earlier → **Tue 2026-09-01**; Wed and Thu are the 2 clear business days between.

**Forward counting ("not more than N days after") — every appeal-lifecycle period.** Unit is plain calendar days, not business days: the Rules write "business days" where they mean them, and the forward periods in Parts 3 and 4 are all in plain days. **Forward algorithm:** (1) count from and including the day *after* the trigger event (Interpretation Act s. 25.2(2)(a) — the reference day precedes the period, so it is excluded); (2) the Nth day so counted is the raw landing day; (3) **if that day is not a business day, extend forward to the next business day** — R. 2: "If the time for doing an act under these rules falls or expires on a day other than a business day, the time is extended to the next business day." Worked check: order pronounced Thu 2026-10-01, N=30 → day 1 = Fri 10-02, day 30 = Sat 10-31, which is not a business day → **Mon 2026-11-02**.

**Forward counting ("at least N business days after") — the earliest-permissible-hearing-date shape.** Added 2026-09-20 (#734) for R. 13(b) and R. 20(4)(b), the only rows in this file with this shape. It is a **floor under a hearing date**, not a deadline to act, so it takes the permissive-marker treatment (same convention as `application_hearing_earliest` in `bcsc-rules.md`, whose forward algorithm this mirrors — minus the court-calendar coverage gate, which has no BCCA analogue). **Forward "at least" algorithm:** (1) count forward from the day after the reference day, incrementing only on business days, until N are counted (Interpretation Act s. 25.2(2)(a)); (2) take **one day later** — s. 25.2(3)**(a)**, which is the forward twin of the s. 25.2(3)(b) adjustment used by the backward algorithm above. Worked check: leave application served Mon 2026-09-28, N=10 → the 10th business day is Wed 2026-10-14 (Sep 30 Truth and Reconciliation and Oct 12 Thanksgiving are both skipped), one day later → **Thu 2026-10-15**. This is a floor, so under the Thanksgiving open question (business-day paragraph above) the cautious treatment is to skip Oct 12 as a holiday, and the stored date stays **Thu 2026-10-15**. If Thanksgiving is a business day, the 10th business day is Tue 2026-10-13 and the floor would be **Wed 2026-10-14**; flag the row, since the two treatments differ. **The s. 25.2(3)(a) added day rests on the same unverified construction as the backward shape — see [reckoning.md](reckoning.md) § Clear-day / exclusive counting, "Assumption, not verified construction (#740)". If s. 25.2 does not reach a business-day period, this floor is one day earlier than shown; a hearing set on or after the stored date is permissible either way, but never call a hearing date too early on a one-day margin.** **#746 confirmed that assumption as permanent — see the backward-count paragraph above and [reckoning.md](reckoning.md). Note that the one registry-practice instance #746 found speaks only to a backward filing row; nothing located touches this forward floor.**

> **Cite s. 25.2(3)(a) here, never (3)(b).** The two paragraphs are each expressly keyed to one counting direction: (3)(a) applies to a result determined under (2)(a) and is "one day later"; (3)(b) applies to a result under (2)(b) and is "one day earlier". Both do the same substantive work — one extra day of clear separation from the reference day, more onerous on the acting party either way — and differ only in sign because the reference day sits on opposite sides of the period. The backward rows in this file cite (3)(b) correctly; this shape must cite (3)(a).

> **The unit of that added day does not matter here, and the reason is worth keeping.** s. 25.2(3)(a) says "one day later" unqualified, leaving open whether it is a calendar or a business day. For this shape the two readings **always agree**: the calendar day after the Nth business day either *is* the (N+1)th business day, or the first business day after it is. Verified by sweeping 400 consecutive trigger dates at N = 10, 5 and 2 — zero divergences (#734). **The equivalence holds because the computed date is a hearing date**, which the registrar can only set on a day the registries are open (R. 83(1)). Do not assume it for a computed *filing* deadline of this shape without re-testing.

> **The landing-day conflict does not reach this shape.** R. 2 is probably not engaged at all: it operates on "the time for doing an act", and R. 13(b) fixes no time for doing the act of obtaining — it constrains the *hearing date* obtained ("hearing date", R. 1(1): "the date set for a hearing"). And the question is moot regardless, because a count expressed in business days cannot land on a non-business day by construction. Even if R. 2 did apply, it and Interpretation Act ss. 25(3)(a)/25(4)(a) all point **forward**, so they agree. The unresolved conflict recorded below arises from ss. 25(3)(b)/25(4)(b), which apply only where the reference day is *after* the period — the backward shape alone.

**Forward counting in *business* days — R. 31(3) only.** One rule in Part 4 runs forward in business days rather than plain days: R. 31(3)'s 2 business days to oppose a request to expedite. Count the same way — from the day *after* the trigger — but count only business days, so the Nth business day so counted is itself the landing day and no R. 2 extension can be needed. Do not generalize this: **R. 31(3) is the only forward-running *maximum* ("not more than") period in these Rules expressed in business days.** R. 13(b) and R. 20(4)(b) also run forward in business days but are *minimum* intervals that put a floor under a hearing date rather than a deadline to act — they take the separate "at least N business days after" algorithm above, including its s. 25.2(3)(a) added day, which this shape does **not** take. Every other business-day period in these Rules counts **backward** from an application hearing date (#736, which scanned the whole regulation for the term).

**Saturday is never a valid landing day under these Rules.** R. 1(1) defines a business day as one on which the registries are "open to the public", and R. 83(1) keeps them open "on each day other than a Saturday or a holiday" — so Saturday fails the definition even though Interpretation Act s. 29 does not name it. R. 83(3) confirms the point structurally: a document submitted on a non-business day is deemed filed on the next business day, so a Saturday landing day would be one on which compliance by filing is impossible. The same result follows from the Interpretation Act alone, without R. 2: for a forward period the reference day precedes the period, so s. 25(4)(a) ends it "on the next day the office is open during its regular business hours". **R. 2 is a s. 2(1) contrary intention displacing ss. 25(3), 25(4) and 25.5 as the *shifting* rule, but nothing else** — ss. 25.2–25.4 still govern counting (including the s. 25.2(3) "at least"/"clear" adjustment), s. 29 still supplies "holiday" for R. 83(1), and the displacement reaches only "an act under these rules". No court has construed R. 2 (#437 note-up: a clean negative, qualified by CanLII's inability to list cases citing a regulation).

**Landing-day policy — R. 2 vs Interpretation Act, unresolved conflict.** R. 2: a time falling on a non-business day "is extended to the **next** business day" (forward). Interpretation Act s. 25(3)(b)/25(4)(b): a backward-running period (reference day after the period) that lands on a holiday/closed day ends on the **previous** open day (backward, preserving the clear notice period). R. 2 is a s. 2(1) "contrary intention" and on its face governs, but a forward shift on a backward-running deadline collapses the "at least" period, and no judicial gloss on R. 2 was located (#433). **Policy: compute both, store the earlier (the backward shift), and flag the deadline for registry confirmation whenever the two diverge** — filing early is never a breach. The conflict bites only when step (3) lands on a non-business day (e.g. a Wednesday hearing); it is dormant for a Thursday or Friday hearing with no intervening holidays.

**The conflict is confined to backward-running deadlines, and it reaches the five plain-days backward rows in this file.** On *forward*-running periods there is no conflict at all: R. 2 and Interpretation Act s. 25(4)(a) both shift forward to the next business day (see Forward counting, above). On backward-running periods the two rules still point opposite ways, and that now affects `bcca_book_of_authorities` (R. 27(2), at least 30 days before the appeal hearing date), `bcca_amended_factum_by_consent` (R. 82(4), at least 4 weeks before it), `bcca_more_justices_request` (R. 43(1), at least 6 weeks before it), `bcca_large_appeal_registrar_directions` (the completion instructions' 90 days) and `bcca_fresh_evidence_application_materials` (R. 59(1), at least 30 days before the appeal hearing date — added by #735) as well as the chambers rows — all are expressed in plain days/weeks rather than business days, so they can land on a non-business day **directly**, where a business-day row reaches one only through the step (3) adjustment. #437 put the question to the gate and it was expressly left unresolved; #736 put R. 43(1) to the gate again and it was again left unresolved; #735 put R. 59(1) to the gate and was told it is the same question as R. 27(2) with no textual difference between them. **All five rows carry this policy and this flag.**

**R. 43(1)'s conflict is deterministic by weekday, unlike R. 27(2)'s.** 6 weeks plus the s. 25.2(3)(b) day is 43 days, and 43 mod 7 = 1, so the raw landing day is always exactly one weekday earlier than the hearing's own weekday. **Every Monday hearing therefore lands the raw date on a Sunday and triggers the conflict**; a Tuesday-to-Friday hearing lands on a business day unless a holiday intervenes. R. 82(4)'s 4 weeks behaves the same way. A 30-day period has no such regularity. Worked check (#736): hearing Mon 2027-03-01 → raw **Sun 2027-01-17**; R. 2 forward gives Mon 01-18, Interpretation Act s. 25(3)(b)/25(4)(b) backward gives Fri 01-15 → **store Fri 2027-01-15 and flag**.

**4 p.m. Pacific cutoffs.** Filing: a document submitted at or before 4 p.m. on a business day is filed that day; after 4 p.m., or on a non-business day, the next business day (R. 83(3); electronic transmission time is the submission time, R. 83(4); registry processing of up to 24 h backdates the stamp, Filing Directive §7.1). Service by email/fax: same 4 p.m. structure (R. 4(3)). Times are Pacific (Interpretation Act s. 26(2)). "File and serve" deadlines have **no separate service date** — R. 3 requires serving a copy of the *filed* version, so both complete the same day. **Rejection headroom:** filing is only deemed on acceptance (R. 83(3) "accepted for filing"; refusal power R. 79(5)) and rejection may surface up to 24 h later — never schedule submission for late on the deadline day.

**What a registry return does to the filing clock (#733, law from #440).** R. 83(3)'s deeming is **conditional on acceptance** — it reaches only "A document submitted for filing to a court registry and accepted for filing by the registrar". A document the registrar has not accepted is outside R. 83(3) entirely: it has **no deemed filing date**, rather than a late one. R. 83(4) fixes submission at transmission, not at registry processing: "A document transmitted for filing electronically is deemed to be submitted when it is transmitted." **No relation-back exists.** No published instrument — the Rules, the Act, or the Registrar's Filing Directive (4 September 2025, read end to end) — addresses resubmission after a registry return or provides relation-back of any kind, so **each transmission is its own submission** and a resubmitted document is timed by its own transmission, never the original's. R. 82 is not relation-back: it governs amending "a document that has been filed". Filing Directive §7.1 is not either: it backdates "to the date they were filed or deemed to be filed", which is R. 83(3)'s own output, so it presupposes acceptance and speaks only to processing lag. **Consequence for every filing deadline in this file:** a package transmitted before 4 p.m. on the deadline day, returned, and retransmitted after 4 p.m. is timed as an after-4-p.m. submission. CA59991 showed it: the response book was transmitted at 3:57 p.m. on the deadline day, returned the same day as a courtesy correction, resubmitted after 4 p.m., and stamped "received not filed". That stamp is not a status any published instrument creates — the Rules' vocabulary is accepted for filing (R. 83(3)) or refused (R. 79(5)) — so what it means is a registry-practice question (`research-findings-440.md` §4 item 1): record it as the practitioner reports it and never characterize it as filed or as refused.

> **Do not encode the CSO courtesy-correction grace period as a rule.** The Court Services Online E-Filing FAQ says a corrected document submitted within two business days keeps the original submission date and time. The FAQ is not an instrument the Rules recognise (the recognised set is the Act and Rules, completion instructions, registrar's directives and practice directives), its own scope text excludes Court of Appeal documents, and the registrar's Filing Directive does not adopt or authorise it. Whether the registry nonetheless applies it to Court of Appeal packages is an open registry-practice question (`research-findings-440.md` §3.4 and §4 items 3–4). Never derive, extend or excuse a date from it.

**The service cut-off is separate from the filing cut-off, and narrower.** R. 4(3)'s 4 p.m. rule reaches only "A document transmitted for service by email or fax". Personal service and delivery to an address for service (R. 4(2)) carry no rule-stated cut-off. R. 3(b) requires serving "a copy of the filed version of the document", so a document not yet accepted for filing has no filed version to serve; what is served in that interval is unanswered by any published source (`research-findings-440.md` §3.5).

**Extension and abridgement.** Act s. 32: a justice may dispense with a requirement of the rules and extend or shorten any time limit, even after it expires. Routes: R. 41 (application to a justice); R. 57 (urgent applications, Form 7 — a justice **or the registrar** may allow shorter notice and set filing/service dates); R. 48(1)(i) (case-management justice); Form 13 consent order to extend time (R. 67). An order for shorter notice may not be varied by the court (R. 62(1)(b)). A resulting order is stored as `source_type='order_term'`, exactly as in the BCSC file.

**Not modelled, because there is nothing to model:** the **condensed-book** lead time, which would exist only if a filing directive imposed one (R. 37(3) — R. 37 itself sets none). #733 read the Registrar's Filing Directive (4 September 2025) for it and §5 imposes none — see "Anchor Types: deadlines set by the Filing Directive and a practice directive". (Moved here from the drafting skills' BCCA response-book reference, 2026-09-17, so no timing sits in drafting context.) #437 modelled the appeal-lifecycle core and split the remainder into its own tickets; add each as a routed row here when modelled.

## Anchor Type: `bcca_application_hearing_date`

The date set for hearing a chambers application before a justice ("hearing date", R. 1(1): "the date set for a hearing"; set by the registrar, R. 56). **Most chambers filing deadlines run backward from it**, and none of *those* runs from the date of service of the notice of application (#433, verified against the full Rules text). Direction for those rows: backward; unit: business days; counting per BCCA Reckoning above; no coverage gate. **Four Division 2 periods are exceptions and run forward instead** — R. 61(2) (14 days from the appellant *filing* its factum), R. 62(2)(a), R. 62(2)(b) and R. 62(4), plus the forward limb of R. 63(2)(b). They are modelled in [§ Anchor Types: chambers applications, Division 2 and adjournments](#anchor-types-chambers-applications-division-2-and-adjournments-r-18-5763-65) and [§ Anchor Types: post-hearing steps and costs](#anchor-types-post-hearing-steps-and-costs), off their own anchors (#735).

**Track split first — there are three tracks, not two** (corrected 2026-09-20, #734). R. 52(1) makes Part 9 Division 1 (Rules 52–56) apply to "every application", **subject to** R. 52(2), which excludes both "(a) an application for leave to appeal **under Rule 13**" and "(c) an application for a stay of proceedings or stay of execution **under Rule 20**". The exclusion operates on the **whole Division**, not on individual rules. So:

- **leave-only track** — R. 13 and R. 14 are self-contained. Excluded by R. 52(2)(a).
- **stay track** (including a stay joined with leave) — R. 20 and R. 21. Excluded by R. 52(2)(c). A *joined* application is not caught by para (a), because R. 20(4) directs the party to proceed "instead of bringing an application for leave to appeal under Rule 13" — para (c) catches it instead. Three exclusion routes, one result: **no Division 1 provision ever applies to leave.**
- **general chambers track** — R. 54 and R. 55, for everything else.

> **Never offer R. 54 or R. 55 on the leave track.** R. 54's 5-business-day applicant deadline and R. 55's *permissive* 2-business-day response are Division 1 provisions, excluded by R. 52(2)(a). A leave applicant's only deadline is R. 13(a); a leave respondent's only route is R. 14(1)'s **mandatory** response book. Storing a `bcca_general_response_materials` row on a leave application would offer a permissive 2-day alternative that does not exist, against a deadline that is actually mandatory at 5 days.

Store only the applicable track's rows:

| Deadline Type | Offset | Rule | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bcca_stay_response_book` | −2 business days (at least / clear) | R. 21(1)(a) + IA s. 25.2(2)(b),(3)(b) | Stay track, not joined with leave: respondent's **response book** (contents per the registrar's completion instructions, R. 21(2) — no prescribed form exists) filed and served. Mandatory once the party elects to respond ("must", R. 21(1)); requires party standing via a filed Form 2 appearance (R. 7, Act s. 1(1)). |
| `bcca_leave_stay_response_book` | −5 business days (at least / clear) | R. 21(1)(b) | Stay track, joined with an application for leave to appeal: respondent's **response book for leave to appeal and stay**. Mutually exclusive with `bcca_stay_response_book`. |
| `bcca_leave_response_book` | −5 business days (at least / clear) | R. 14(1) | **Leave-only track**: "A party who wishes to respond to an application for leave to appeal must file and serve a **response book for leave to appeal** at least 5 business days before the application hearing date." **Conditional-mandatory** — the deadline does not exist until the party elects to respond ("who wishes to"), and on that election it is "**must**", with no permissive alternative. Binds **"a party"**, not only the named respondent. Mutually exclusive with both stay-track response books. Contents per the registrar's completion instructions (R. 14(2)) — no prescribed form. |
| `bcca_application_materials` | −5 business days (at least / clear) | R. 20(3) (stay track) / R. 54 (general chambers) | Applicant's filing-and-service deadline: Form 4 notice of application + application book (stay track, R. 20(3)) or Form 4 + supporting affidavits + written argument (general chambers, R. 54). Latest permissible day to file and serve — a missed date is a real default. |
| `bcca_general_response_materials` | −2 business days (at least / clear) | R. 55 | General chambers track only: respondent's supporting affidavits and written argument. **Permissive** ("may") — record that in `rule_description`. Mutually exclusive with the two response-book rows. |

- All five are **file-and-serve by 4 p.m. Pacific** deadlines (BCCA Reckoning, above).
- **The "must" in R. 14(1) is deliberate drafting, not loose usage**, and the contrast within these Rules proves it: R. 55, R. 58(2), R. 59(2) and R. 60(3) all use "**may** … file and serve … at least *n* business days before the application hearing date" for genuinely optional responses. R. 14(1) does not. Record it as mandatory-on-election, never as permissive.
- **The Rules attach no consequence to not filing a leave response book.** That silence is significant, because the drafter supplied an express consequence where it wanted one — R. 7(2) and R. 10(2) both presume a non-filing party "takes no position" and relieve others from serving further documents. R. 14 has no analogue. Whether a non-responding party may still appear and be heard **is not answerable from the rule text** (#734 gate closed) — do not infer it.
- A stay of execution / stay of proceedings under Act s. 33 via R. 20 takes `bcca_stay_response_book` (or the leave-joined row). Relief framed under Act s. 30(c) (interim orders to prevent prejudice) is arguably outside R. 20(1) and on the R. 55 track instead — **same −2 business-day arithmetic either way**; the difference is the responding document, not the date. When an application mixes both heads, store the response-book row (it satisfies both descriptions) and surface the characterization to the practitioner (#433 memo §6).
- Cross-examination on affidavits needs no stored row: R. 44(3) requires only that it occur before the hearing, with no lead time prescribed.

## Anchor Type: `bcca_application_service_date`

The date a BCCA notice of application is served. **Fact-only anchor** — store the anchor and its source, derive **no** deadline rows from it: every chambers response deadline in these Rules anchors to the hearing date, not to service (#433 — R. 21(1), R. 55 both read "before the application hearing date"). **One exception, and it is not an exception to this anchor: R. 62(4)** runs forward from service of the **application book**, never from service of the notice of application, so it takes its own anchor `bcca_vary_justice_order_application_book_served` (#735) and still derives nothing from this one. The service date remains worth storing as the trigger fact (it starts the practical response window and evidences R. 20(3)/R. 54 compliance by the applicant). Never invent a service-anchored countdown; when the hearing date is known, store a `bcca_application_hearing_date` anchor and derive from that.

## Anchor Types: applying for leave to appeal

Modelled 2026-09-20 (#734). This is everything **upstream** of the `bcca_leave_to_appeal_granted` anchor in the next section — the application for leave itself, its hearing-date floor, and its response. Reuse that anchor when leave is granted; never create a second one.

**When leave is required at all.** R. 12: a party bringing an appeal or cross appeal **must** apply for leave if (a) the order is a "limited appeal order" (defined by the closed list in R. 11), (b) another enactment requires leave, or (c) **the party does not know whether leave is required**. Limb (c) matters for intake — uncertainty itself triggers the obligation, so a practitioner who is unsure is already inside R. 13. Whether a given order is a limited appeal order is a legal characterization, not something this skill computes: **ask, never classify.**

**Two tracks, mutually exclusive, numerically identical.** R. 20(4) opens "To join a stay application with an application for leave to appeal, a party must, **instead of** bringing an application for leave to appeal under Rule 13…", and then reproduces both of R. 13's paragraphs with one change of document name. R. 13 has no other content, so nothing of it survives the substitution. Store one track or the other, never both.

> **The periods are identical, so a mis-cite is invisible in the date and visible only in the authority line.** Citing R. 13 for a joined leave-and-stay application yields the right day under the wrong rule — the same trap as R. 82(6) vs R. 17(8) below. The track decides the citation *and* the document: "application book for leave to appeal" (R. 13(a)) or "application book for leave to appeal **and stay**" (R. 20(4)(a)).

### The application

File **and** serve a Form 4 notice of application and an application book for leave to appeal. Trigger is **filing**, not service or pronouncement. Mandatory once R. 12 bites |

**The trigger act is deliberately chosen, and these Rules use three different ones.** R. 6(2)(a) runs from the order being **pronounced**; R. 7(1) and R. 9 run from **being served**; R. 13(a) and R. 20(4)(a) run from **filing**. Do not substitute one for another. Note also that R. 13 contains **two different reference events**: paragraph (a) counts from filing the notice of appeal or cross appeal, paragraph (b) from filing and service of *the leave application itself*.

**Which filing act does "the related notice of appeal or notice of cross appeal, as applicable" name?** The instrument that commenced **the applicant's own** proceeding — R. 12 defines the leave applicant as "a party bringing an appeal or cross appeal". An appellant seeking leave counts from its own R. 6 filing; a cross-appellant from its own R. 9 filing. **Not** "whichever came first", and **not** the notice of appeal in every case. That is why `bcca_notice_of_cross_appeal_filed` exists as its own anchor — the pre-existing `bcca_notice_of_cross_appeal_served` anchor records a different act and cannot stand in for it.

**One application never has two candidate trigger dates.** What the Rules contemplate instead is two *separate* applications with two separate triggers, forced to one hearing by R. 15. R. 15 supplies no rule for reconciling them, and a justice or the registrar may order otherwise.

### The hearing-date floor is a floor, not a deadline

R. 13(b) reads: "obtain a hearing date for the application **that is** at least 10 business days after the application for leave to appeal is filed and served." The relative clause modifies "**a hearing date**", not the verb "obtain". So the rule does two different things, and only one of them is a date this skill stores:

- **A qualifying condition on the date obtained** — the floor. Stored as `bcca_leave_hearing_earliest`, a permissive marker. Record that in `rule_description` so `executive-assistant` and query-deadlines do not treat it as an obligation.
- **An untimed duty to obtain a hearing date.** Mandatory ("must … obtain"), but **the rule states no date by which it must be discharged**, and R. 1(1) defines "obtain a hearing date" as obtaining one **from the registrar** — an act the applicant requests but does not control. **It gets no row**, because a dateless obligation has no row shape in a date store; it is recorded here instead. Surface it in the summary when the floor row is stored, so the duty is visible without being fabricated as a deadline.

Two structural confirmations that this is a floor rather than a deadline: R. 53 requires a hearing date **before filing**, but R. 52(2)(a) and (c) exclude leave and stay applications from that Division — and consistently, R. 13(b) computes *from* filing and service, which presupposes filing may come first. Contrast R. 18(3), which imports the R. 53 sequence **expressly** for added-respondent applications. Meanwhile the true backward deadlines in these Rules (R. 14(1), R. 20(3), R. 54) are all drafted "at least N business days **before** the application hearing date" — a shape R. 13(b) does not share.

**The count runs from service, and the reason is structural.** The trigger is conjunctive — "is filed **and** served" — and R. 3 requires serving "a copy of **the filed version**" of the document, so service can never precede filing. Service is therefore always the later act or the same day, and it supplies the reference day. Each component day is itself fixed by a deeming rule to apply first: **R. 83(3)** for filing and **R. 4(3)**, identically worded, for service by email or fax — at or before 4 p.m. on a business day, that day; otherwise the next business day.

> **Do not generalize the "service is later" reasoning to other conjunctive triggers.** It comes from R. 3's "filed version" requirement, not from any general rule about two-act triggers. Check that R. 3 governs before reusing it. Where several parties are served on different days, the text does not say whether the count runs from the last service or per party — **#734 gate closed, ask the practitioner.**

### The response

The respondent's step is in the chambers section above, because it anchors to the hearing date rather than to anything here: `bcca_leave_response_book` (R. 14(1), leave-only) or `bcca_leave_stay_response_book` (R. 21(1)(b), joined). Same 5-business-day period, different document, different rule — the track decides.

### Displacing these periods

Both periods are variable by order, and the enabling power is in the **Act**, not the Rules. Act s. 32(2) lets a justice extend **or shorten** a time limit "**including the time limit for commencing an appeal or application for leave to appeal**", and s. 32(3) preserves that power even after expiry. Procedural routes: **R. 41** (extension only — the shortening power lives in s. 32(2) itself); **R. 57(3)**, where a justice **or the registrar** may order an application heard on shorter notice and set both the hearing date and the filing/service dates; **R. 48(1)(c)/(i)** in case management, which a justice may exercise with or without any application; and **R. 67(2)(a) with Form 13**, the consent order — note its scope is "an order to extend the time to file a document", which reaches R. 13(a) but does not in terms reach the R. 13(b) floor. Store any resulting order as `source_type='order_term'` and prefer it over the rule row.

**The registrar has no general power to extend time** — R. 41 names a justice only, and Act s. 32 confers the power on a justice. The registrar reaches these periods only through R. 57(3) or, in case management, R. 48(1) (authorized by the Registrar's Powers Civil Practice Directive, 3 May 2024, under Act s. 37(2)). Any registrar order is in turn variable by a justice (Act s. 35(2)).

> **One re-variation limit worth flagging, not asserting.** Act s. 35(1)(b) lets a justice vary an order made under s. 32(2) **other than** one "extending or shortening the time limit for commencing an appeal or application for leave to appeal". Reading Act s. 15 with s. 32(2) and s. 35(1)(b) suggests R. 13(a) is that limit, so an order varying it would not be re-variable — but that is a textual chain with **no authority confirming it** (#734). Surface it; do not assert it.

**On the required Saturday landing.** Every forward period in this section is 7 or 14 days — a
multiple of 7 — so its raw landing day always falls on the **same weekday as its trigger**. A Saturday
landing is therefore unreachable from any of these rows unless the trigger itself falls on a Saturday,
which for a filing trigger it cannot (R. 83(1), R. 83(3)(b)(ii)). The forward algorithm's Saturday
behaviour is exercised instead by the appeal-lifecycle rows, whose periods are not multiples of 7:
#437 §6 examples 1 and 5 (order pronounced Thu 2026-10-01, +30 days → Sat 2026-10-31 → **Mon
2026-11-02**; notice served Fri 2026-10-09, +15 days → Sat 2026-10-24 → **Mon 2026-10-26**). The
algorithm is shared, so no separate Saturday example is constructed here rather than fabricating a
Saturday trigger.

**One arithmetic consequence worth naming.** Examples 2 and 3 show that a **business-day** backward row
*can* land on a non-business day and so *can* engage the landing-day conflict — not because the
business-day count reaches one, but because step (3), the s. 25.2(3)(b) "at least" adjustment, takes
one further **calendar** day earlier. This is what § BCCA Reckoning already says ("the conflict bites
only when step (3) lands on a non-business day, e.g. a Wednesday hearing"), and it is why the chambers
rows carry the policy too.

## Anchor Types: the appeal lifecycle

Re-derived in full 2026-09-20 (#437), replacing two thin 2026-07-11 rows that collapsed three separate filings into one, carried no computable offset, modelled the appeal book as unconditional, modelled no respondent-side step at all, and applied the wrong landing-day rule.

**The cascade chains off observed events, never off due dates.** R. 25(1) gives the appellant 30 days "after **filing the appeal record**" — the actual filing. File the record on day 40 rather than day 60 and the factum falls due 30 days after day 40. So each step is its own anchor recorded when it happens, and the appellant's factum can never be modelled as "90 days from the notice of appeal". Store each anchor as the practitioner reports the event; derive only the rows whose anchor exists.

**Filing and service are different trigger acts, and adjacent rules use different ones** — R. 25(2) runs from *service* of the appellant's factum, R. 26(5)(a) from *filing* of the respondent's factum. Never merge them. In practice a "file and serve" obligation usually completes both on one day (R. 3 requires serving a copy of the *filed* version), but where the practitioner reports different dates, use the act the rule names.

**R. 22 starts no clock.** It defines when an appeal is "brought"; no Part 4 rule uses "brought" as a trigger. Store it as a status fact if at all, and never derive a Part 4 date from it.

### The three appeal books — conditional, and with no period of their own

R. 26(2) does not set a period. It sets **simultaneity**, with **both** acts: the book is due "when the [party] files and serves that factum". And the obligation exists only if its condition is met. Store these as presence-keyed rows carrying the factum's own date, and store **no row at all** when the condition fails.

| Deadline Type | Condition (both limbs must hold where two are listed) | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `bcca_appellants_appeal_book` | the appellant's factum refers to evidence | R. 26(2) |
| `bcca_respondents_appeal_book` | the respondent's factum refers to evidence **and** that evidence is not in the appellant's appeal book | R. 26(3) |
| `bcca_respondents_cross_appeal_appeal_book` | the cross appeal factum refers to evidence **and** that evidence is not in the appellant's appeal book | R. 30(2)(a) |

Whether the condition holds is a fact about the draft factum, not something this skill can compute — **ask the practitioner; never assume the book is required.** Each is discharged in the alternative by a joint appeal book (R. 26(5)(b), R. 30(2)(b)).

### Two rules, same number: R. 82(6) and R. 17(8)

Both give 15 days from service of an amended factum, and they are **different rules**. R. 17(10): "Rule 82 … does not apply to an amended notice of appeal, amended factum or amended response factum that is filed under this rule." Their displacement clauses differ too — R. 82(6) reads "Unless otherwise directed by **the court**, a justice or the registrar"; R. 17(8) names only "a justice or the registrar". Cite R. 17(8) when the amendment arises from appealing a subsequent related order under R. 17; cite R. 82(6) otherwise. Storing one rule for both mis-cites every instance of the other.

## Anchor Types: hearing steps, adjournment, the inactive list and abandonment

Modelled 2026-09-20 (#736), graduating the cluster #437 left unmodelled: R. 31–35, 37, 43, 45, 46 and 49–51, plus the one timing the e-filing completion instructions carry. Three `/legal-research` dispatches, all saved in `Engineering/bcca-appeal-books-inputs/`; full record in `research-findings-736.md`. **Reuse `bcca_appeal_hearing_date` — never create a second hearing-date anchor.** It already exists (created by #437 for the R. 27(2) book of authorities). Three routes put a date into it: the appellant obtaining one (R. 33(1)(a)), the respondent obtaining one (R. 34(1)), and the registrar setting one (R. 35(1)). **Whether the registrar's setting is the operative date-fixing act, as against a date "obtained" by a party, is an open gate** (#736) — record the date the practitioner reports and do not characterize the mechanism. R. 35(1) and R. 45 both move this anchor, so a change to it cascades to every row that depends on it.

**"Obtaining a hearing date" is a different event from the hearing date.** `bcca_hearing_date_obtained_by_respondent` records the day the respondent secured the date; `bcca_appeal_hearing_date` records the day the hearing will happen. R. 34(2)'s 5 days run from the former and will ordinarily expire long before the latter. Never collapse them.

### R. 45(2) — a threshold, not a deadline

`bcca_adjournment_written_request_threshold`, computed from `bcca_appeal_hearing_date` as **hearing date − 21 days**, is the day the adjournment procedure changes. It is **not a due date and nothing is due on it**. Store it with that label in `rule_description` so no consumer treats it as an obligation.

Outside the window, R. 45(1) requires only that a party "notify the registrar without delay". Inside it — "during the 3 weeks before the appeal hearing date" — R. 45(2) requires a **written request for the adjournment** submitted to the registrar. What changes on crossing the boundary is the instrument, not a deadline: R. 45(2) fixes no date by which anything must be done, and R. 45(1)'s own standard is uncountable.

Because it is a window boundary and not an "at least" period, it takes **no s. 25.2(3)(b) adjustment and no landing-day shift** — a boundary is not a "time for doing an act" that expires. Count it under s. 25.2(2)(b) alone: day 1 is the day before the hearing date, day 21 is the boundary. Worked check: hearing Wed 2027-03-10 → window opens **Wed 2027-02-17** and runs to Tue 2027-03-09 inclusive.

### The inactive appeal list and dismissal as abandoned

**The `bcca_appeal_ready_for_hearing` anchor (R. 32).** An appeal is ready for hearing on either (a) the date the appellant has filed **both** its factum and a copy of each order being appealed as entered below, or (b) the date a justice or the registrar specifies. Limb (a) is conjunctive, so the anchor date is the **later of the two filings**, not the first. R. 32 is a status definition and runs no clock of its own — the one rule in these Rules that counts from it is R. 50(1)(b). Because limb (b) lets an official set the date outright, this anchor is not always derivable from the filing record: ask the practitioner rather than inferring it.

**Selecting between R. 50(1)(a) and (b).** The two are **alternative, not cumulative** — the disjunctive "or", and the singular "the date" governing both. One duty, two candidate computations. **Which governs when both are computable is an open gate** (#736): the text supports both an "earliest-applicable" reading and a "circumstance-selected" reading ((b) once the appeal is ready for hearing, (a) otherwise), and no authority resolves it. Derive whichever computations their anchors support; where both exist, **store the earlier and flag the row that the (a)/(b) selection is unresolved** — the same compute-both-store-the-earlier idiom the landing-day policy uses. In practice the readings converge at both ends: an appeal prosecuted on time is ready for hearing near day 90 and so (b) lands near day 150, well inside (a)'s year; an appeal whose appellant never files a factum never becomes ready for hearing under R. 32(a), so (b) never starts and (a) governs alone. They diverge only in a narrow middle band.

**"One year" is anniversary reckoning, not 365 days.** R. 50(1)(a) routes through Interpretation Act **s. 25.4(1)(a)**, not s. 25.2: go forward to the numerically corresponding date in the same calendar month of the following year. s. 25.4(2) does **not** apply — "one year after" is not "clear", "at least" or "not less than" — so there is no added day. s. 25.4(3) sends a 29 February anniversary to 28 February in a non-leap year. Worked check: notice of appeal filed Tue 2028-02-29 → **Wed 2029-02-28**.

**These rows do not take the ordinary forward shift, and that is the exception to the file's general rule.**

- **R. 51(1) shifts under no provision at all.** All four candidate shifting rules are expressly limited to *doing an act* — R. 2 ("the time for doing an act under these rules"), Interpretation Act ss. 25(3) and 25(4) ("a period for doing an act"), and s. 25.5(1) ("a day that is specified for doing an act"). R. 51(1) prescribes no act by anyone; the appeal simply "remains" on the list. **Store the raw date wherever it falls, Saturday and holidays included.** This bites often: 180 days from a Monday placement always lands on a Saturday. Worked check: placed Mon 2026-12-07 → **Sat 2027-06-05**, stored as a Saturday.
- **R. 50(1) and R. 50(4)(b) are an open gate.** An act *is* in view — a party's filing — but it sits inside a provision whose operative verb is "The registrar **must place**" / "**must return**". Whether R. 2 reaches a date that crystallizes a registrar's duty rather than fixing a party's own deadline is unresolved, and the Interpretation Act carries the same "doing an act" limitation. **Store the unshifted date with the shift flagged unresolved**; do not bake in either reading. The divergence is one to three days. Worked check: ready for hearing Tue 2027-04-06 → day 60 = **Sat 2027-06-05**, stored unshifted and flagged.

**The R. 50(5) notice is not a reliable source for the placement date.** The registrar must "provide notice" on placing or returning an appeal, but the subrule prescribes no content (it need not state the date), no form, and no timing, and it reaches only parties who have provided an address for service. Treat the computed R. 50(1) date as the working figure, and confirm the actual placement date with the registry before relying on a R. 51(1) count — the placement is a registrar's act that may not occur on the computed day, and R. 51(1) runs from the appeal *being on* the list, not from the day it should have been placed there.

### Provenance: the 4,000-page, 90-day item is registry practice, not a rule

Store `bcca_large_appeal_registrar_directions` with its source recorded as the **e-filing completion instructions** and their retrieval date — **not** as a rule-sourced deadline citing R. 23(2), R. 26(4)(a) or R. 27(4). Those provisions incorporate the completion instructions only for how a document "must be **prepared**", and the R. 1(1) definition matches ("instructions … for completion of documents"). A duty to *seek directions from the Registrar* on a timetable keyed to the hearing date is not preparation, and the passage sits among the e-filing file-size bullets rather than the content or format items. So on the text of the incorporating rules the 90 days is **not** brought into the Rules (#736).

That is not a finding that it does not bind. Court of Appeal Act s. 38(1) lets the registrar issue filing directives "including … instructions for the completion of documents", and s. 38(2) permits refusal to accept a document filed contrary to one. Whether these completion instructions are a s. 38(1) directive, and whether this item is one "respecting the filing of documents", is an **open gate**. Diarize the 90 days either way — the practical consequence of reaching a 4,000-page appeal without registrar directions is the same on both characterizations. Miscoding its provenance is the error that matters.

## Anchor Types: deadlines set by the Filing Directive and a practice directive

Modelled 2026-09-21 (#733), handed over by #440 (`Engineering/bcca-response-inputs/research-findings-440.md` §3.7 and §6.2). These two rows are the only ones in this file whose period comes from neither the Rules nor the completion instructions. The Registrar's Filing Directive is made under Act s. 38 and R. 5; the Remote Appearances practice directive under R. 89. Instrument text: `Engineering/bcca-response-inputs/coa_filing_directive.txt` and `pd_remote_appearances.txt`. No new anchor is needed — every anchor below already exists.

| `bcca_appeal_record_filed`, `bcca_appellants_factum_filed`, `bcca_respondents_factum_filed`, `bcca_respondents_cross_appeal_factum_filed` — whichever the **client** e-filed | `bcca_paper_copies_followup` | +2 weeks | Filing Directive §4 (4 September 2025) | "If e-filed (required for lawyers in civil appeals) you must provide the registry with three (3) stamped paper copies of the e-filed document within two (2) weeks of e-filing." §4 applies it to two classes: "Appeal records" and "Factums and statements". **Binds the filing party only** — derive it from a filed anchor only where the client is the party who filed, never from an opposing party's filing. ### Counting — both rows carry an assumption, stored in the cautious direction

Neither directive says how its period is reckoned. Whether the Interpretation Act's counting sections or R. 2's landing-day extension ("the time for doing an act under these rules") reach a period fixed by a directive was put to the `/legal-research` gate on 2026-09-21 (#753, under the CA59991 anchor). The gate returned **mixed coverage: no located authority decides either point, either way.** Answer saved in the matter's `6. LAW\BCCA business-day counting\2026-09-21 research-lawyer answer.md`. What it found on the text:

- **Whether a directive is an "enactment" is open, not settled against.** Interpretation Act s. 1 defines "regulation" to include an "other instrument enacted (a) in execution of a power conferred under an Act", and "enact" includes "to issue". On those words there is a serious argument that both directives are enactments that s. 2(1) reaches. No authority applies the definition to a court directive. Never write that a directive "is not an enactment" as though that were established.
- **R. 2 is limited by its own words** to "an act under these rules". R. 1(1) "file" (which takes in "any directives of the registrar issued under section 38") and Act s. 38(1) ("Subject to the rules") are textual bridges for the Filing Directive, and neither is conclusive. The Remote Appearances directive is made under R. 89 and has no such bridge.

Both rows therefore still store the date that cannot be late under any reading, and say so in `rule_description`:

- **`bcca_paper_copies_followup`:** count 14 days from and including the day after the e-filing day. If day 14 is not a business day, store the **preceding** business day and flag the row. **This stored date assumes the no-extension branch** — that neither R. 2 nor the Interpretation Act reaches the directive, so day 14 stands as the last day and the registry cannot receive paper on a closed day. No located rule moves day 14 *backward* as a matter of law: the preceding business day is practical compliance, not a legal shift. Every candidate rule runs **forward** — R. 2 ("extended to the next business day"), IA s. 25(3)(a) (holiday) and s. 25(4)(a) (office closed). The backward branches, s. 25(3)(b) and s. 25(4)(b), need the reference day to be after the period, and here it is before. So the other reading is the next business day *after* day 14, which can be more than one day later than the stored date. **Never call paper copies late on that margin.** E-filing on a business day lands day 14 on the same weekday, so the adjustment bites only when day 14 is a holiday or the document was deemed filed on a day other than its transmission day. Worked check: factum e-filed Tue 2026-10-13 → day 14 is **Tue 2026-10-27**, a business day, stored as is.
- **`bcca_request_to_appear_remotely`:** use the backward algorithm in BCCA Reckoning **including its step (3)**, so ten full business days lie clear between the filing day and the hearing day. "No later than" is not one of the s. 25.2(3) trigger phrases ("at least", "not less than", "clear"), so step (3) may not be required here. The gate's answer (#753) reads that trigger list as closed and calls dropping the extra day the better reading of the text, on three points: "no later than" is not a trigger phrase, whether a business-day period is "a period described in subsection (2)" at all is undecided, and s. 25.2(3) never arises if the Act does not reach a practice directive. No authority supports or contradicts that reading. Step (3) is kept because the stored date is then safe either way, at the cost of one day. **Never call a Request late on that one-day margin.** A business-day count cannot land on a non-business day except through step (3); if it does, apply the landing-day policy (store the earlier date, flag). Worked check: hearing Mon 2026-11-23 → counting back from Fri 11-20, skipping Remembrance Day (Wed 11-11), the tenth business day is Fri 11-06; one day earlier → **Thu 2026-11-05**.

### Carries no deadline row

- **Request to Appear Remotely on the chambers track.** Part 1 item 2 of the same directive: "For Chambers: File a Request to Appear Remotely with their application materials or responding materials in accordance with the Chambers Applications practice directive." It carries no period of its own and rides the deadline of the materials it accompanies — the `bcca_application_hearing_date` rows above. Never store a separate date for it.
- **Condensed books.** Filing Directive §5 lets parties "hand up paper condensed books at the appeal hearing or may file paper copies in advance if they will appear by Zoom", and sets no lead time. That closes the question BCCA Reckoning left open under R. 37(3): the Directive imposes none.

## Anchor Types: post-hearing steps and costs

Modelled 2026-09-20 (#737), graduating R. 38–R. 40 (Part 7), R. 69–R. 78 (Part 11) and R. 86–R. 88. Three `/legal-research` dispatches, all saved in `Engineering/bcca-appeal-books-inputs/`; full record in `research-findings-737.md`. All three found the shared commons pool **empty** on BC Court of Appeal procedural timing — six consecutive dispatches now, counting #437's three.

**This cluster is almost entirely deadline-free, and that is the finding, not an absence of one.** Fourteen rules were examined and twelve carry no period in any form — not a day count, not a "without delay"/"promptly" standard, not an event-tied period, not an imported one. Each is recorded in [Carries no deadline row](#carries-no-deadline-row) above **with its reason**, so this negative is never re-derived. Only two rows exist, below.

**Part 7 is pronouncement-anchored throughout.** R. 38, R. 39(1) and R. 40(1) each open with the identical formula "After judgment on appeal has been given". None runs from filing or from service. **Those words are an opening trigger with no terminus** — they mark when an act may be done, not when it must be. A row counting a period from judgment would be inventing one.

### The two rows

| Anchor Type | Deadline Type | Offset | Rule | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bcca_registrar_order_date` — the registrar's order or direction is made, **including the issuing of a certificate of costs** (R. 63(1)) | `bcca_vary_registrar_order` | +7 days | R. 63(2)(b) | **Mandatory** ("must"), rights-forfeiting. Apply to a justice under Act s. 35 to vary or cancel. File **and** serve a Form 4 notice of application, supporting affidavits and written argument. Plain days, forward. **Subject to a second, backward constraint — see below** |
| `bcca_lawyer_withdrawal_served` — a Form 19 notice of withdrawal of lawyer is **served** | `bcca_withdrawal_objection` | +7 days | R. 87(2) | **Permissive** ("may object") — record that in `rule_description`. File **and** serve an objection in Form 20. Plain days, forward. Expiry has a hard downstream effect: R. 88(2)(b), R. 88(3) |

Both reuse the **forward algorithm** in BCCA Reckoning unchanged. Neither takes the landing-day conflict flag: both run forward, where R. 2 and Interpretation Act s. 25(4)(a) agree.

### R. 63 is the BCCA's costs-review window, and it is 7 days where the BCSC's is 14

**This is the most important thing in this section.** The review window for a costs assessment is not in Part 11 at all. R. 63(1) provides: "In this rule, 'order' **includes a certificate of costs issued by the registrar**." So the certificate feeds R. 63's 7 days.

> **Never carry the BCSC number across.** BCSC Rule 14-1(29) gives **14 days** to apply for a review of a registrar's decision on an assessment. The BCCA gives **7**. A model reasoning by analogy from `bcsc-rules.md` would be wrong by a week, in the direction that forfeits the client's right of review.

**R. 63(2)(b) imposes two constraints on one filing, and they can be unsatisfiable.** The same documents must be filed and served:

- **not more than 7 days after** the order was made **or direction given** — forward, plain days; **and**
- **at least 5 business days before** the application hearing date — backward, business days.

The backward limb is arithmetically identical to `bcca_application_materials` (R. 54, −5 business days), so **no new backward row is created** — that row already does the work once a `bcca_application_hearing_date` exists. What is new is the forward cap, which the chambers machinery does not otherwise impose. R. 63(3)'s response limb is likewise the existing `bcca_general_response_materials` arithmetic (−2 business days).

**R. 63(2)(a) requires the hearing date to be obtained *first*.** An applicant who follows the rule in order and takes too early a date destroys their own ability to comply. **Compute both limbs, store the earlier, and flag the row as infeasible where the backward limb falls before the anchor date** — do not silently store a date in that case.

### R. 87(2) binds each person served, not only the client

R. 87(1) does not say on whom the Form 19 is served. R. 3 supplies "each party to the appeal". So the 7 days is **per served person**, each running from that person's own service date, and the model must be able to carry more than one row. Service dates can genuinely differ, because R. 4(3) deems an email or fax transmitted after 4 p.m. or on a non-business day to be served on the next business day.

**Expiry does real work, and the effect lives in R. 88.** R. 88(2)(b): a lawyer ceases to be lawyer of record when "the lawyer files and serves a notice under Rule 87 (1) **and no objection is made under Rule 87 (2)**". R. 88(3): documents may be served on a lawyer as another's lawyer of record **until** that cessation. So the expiry date governs whether service on the former lawyer is good service. Where an objection *is* made, R. 88(2)(c) replaces the computed date with an event — a court declaration — so the row stops being derivable.

### R. 39 needs no new arithmetic

A costs-assessment application runs on the **ordinary chambers clock** already modelled. R. 39(2) requires compliance with R. 53 and R. 54 and adds one thing: a **bill of costs in Form 21 attached to the notice of application**, which therefore rides the notice's own due date. Two footings confirm the chambers machinery applies: R. 52(1) extends Part 9 Division 1 to "every application made to the court, a justice **or the registrar**", and R. 52(2) excludes a registrar application where the drafters wanted to (para. (e), R. 68) but does **not** exclude R. 39.

So: store `bcca_application_materials` (−5 business days, R. 54) on the general chambers track, and note the Form 21 attachment. **Create no R. 39-specific row.** There is **no outer limit** on when an assessment may be sought — R. 39(1) fixes only an opening event.

### `order_pronounced` in the BCCA is fact-only as to costs

Nothing runs from the pronouncement or entry of a Court of Appeal order that bears on costs: R. 39(1) and R. 40(1) both open the door and never shut it, and neither the Rules nor the Act supplies an outer limit. The existing BCCA `order_pronounced` anchor therefore derives **nothing on costs**.

It is **not inert**, though. A single justice's order is variable under R. 62, which carries three mandatory periods — 7 days to file a Form 8, 14 days from that filing for an application book, and 7 days from service for a response book. **R. 62 is not modelled here**: it sits in Part 9 Division 2 (R. 57–R. 63), which belongs to [#735](https://github.com/Mharbulous/CoClerk/issues/735). The anchor above is named `bcca_registrar_order_date` rather than narrowly for costs precisely so #735 can extend it to every registrar order and direction without renaming or duplicating.
