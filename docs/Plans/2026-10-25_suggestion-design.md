# Suggestion mechanism: first-principles design discussion

Status: Q1 to Q6 settled (2026-10-25). Reviewed by Fable three times. Brahm ruled on the third review's reversals: the click-time prompt (8, 28), one mockup link (24) and withdrawal without a purge (16) were adopted, and `technical-writer` stays on the practitioner's machine with fixes (18). Existing /lawyer:suggestion not read (until "now compare").

## Given (from the brief)
- Practitioners are busy and non-technical. They won't know what the tool can do unless it offers.
- Suggestions go to the Supabase suggestion box, which maintainers triage. Maintainers can't easily ask follow-ups.
- Nothing identifying a client, matter, person, document or file path leaves the machine.
- Sources: the practitioner directly, or the AI noticing a pain point during work.
- Motivating example: one suggestion bundled one pain point (a structured list in white on dark green, flat indentation) with four items: numbers in their own column with hanging indents; connectors like "or alternatively" on their own italic lines; black serif text on a light background; and the constraint "no nested cards or boxes", found only when a first mockup was rejected as "too graphical". It was posted as one paragraph with no mockup.
- Probanda's MCP server already serves localhost pages that receive clicks (key-request, mail, timecamp, housekeeping, page-api).

## Goal
A suggestion exists so that a pain point, whether stated or not and whether felt or not, reaches the people who change Probanda with enough detail that they can fix it, or decide not to, without going back to the practitioner. It passes when (a) a maintainer can accept or decline each item in it without asking the practitioner anything (measured on the maintainer's side as the share of items declined as "not enough detail", decision 17), and (b) someone who knows the firm's clients can't tell which client, matter, person, document or file it came from. Test (b) is the standard the scrub works to. Its automatic test is a cross-check that the payload holds no token found in the local client, matter, contact, folder-name, document-title or file-name records (decision 8). Test (b) itself is applied only by the maintainer at triage (decision 12, layer 3). The practitioner can later see exactly what was sent, withdraw it, and see what happened to it.

## Decisions

Audiences and scope
1. Audiences: the practitioner, the maintainer (whoever changes Probanda's source), and whoever triages many suggestions together.
2. Every suggestion has a pain point. When it is unstated, the card always shows a best guess labelled "our guess, edit if wrong", in the pain-point position (decision 6). Nothing is asked in chat.
3. Pain the practitioner never felt counts, for example cost, reliability, accuracy and confidentiality near-misses. An AI-noticed card appears at the next reply in which the session would otherwise wait for the practitioner after the observation, at most one per day across sessions. It never appears for a fingerprint the practitioner dismissed, sent or left unanswered within the last 30 days, or at any time while the plugin version is the one it was dismissed, sent or left unanswered under. A card left unanswered when the session ends counts as dismissed. It carries the raw observation, taken only from Probanda's own output (token counts, tool names, error classes or codes, the plugin's own fixed text), never argument values, error message bodies, client documents or the practitioner's words, and a fingerprint (plugin, version, skill, kind). The decision 12 scrub runs over the raw observation before the card is shown. Merging by fingerprint is a triage view over separate records, never one merged record.

Terms
4. One practitioner-facing term, **Suggestion**, whoever started it. The card opens with "Probanda noticed:" or "You suggested:". Origin is also a field for triage.

The card (`Suggestion-<id>.html`, shown as a file card, served by Probanda's local server)
5. The chat shows no suggestion talk at all. Its only sign is the file card. A mockup is a second file card.
6. The card, in order: the origin cue; the pain point, described by its effect on the practitioner, felt or not; the items (decision 23), then the "Show a mockup of this" link (decision 24); the constraint and evidence lines, the raw observation among them; an optional comment box with the grey placeholder "Please don't name clients, matters, people or files."; the line "Probanda receives this from your account: what you see here, a technical write-up of it, and technical details about the Probanda software on this computer." with a "Show technical details" link (environment facts); the line "You can see this later, with its status, under Past suggestions."; then **Submit Suggestion** and **Dismiss**.
7. The pain point, each item and each context line are editable in place before sending. Emptying an item removes it.
8. On click, everything sent (decision 28) is checked by the local cross-check plus patterns for paths, emails, dates and amounts. If a token from the local records, an email address or a path matches in text the practitioner can read on the card, a one-line prompt offers Remove only; the practitioner may also retype the text in place. If a date or an amount matches there, it offers Remove and Send anyway. The cross-check's tokens are the words of three or more letters, and the numbers of four or more digits, in the local client, matter, contact, folder-name, document-title and file-name records, less a shipped stop list of generic legal and folder words, common English and the firm's own name. Matching is whole-word and ignores case.
9. Dismiss sends nothing, is recorded locally (for the suppression in decision 3), and closes the card. If the card cannot be closed, Dismiss greys it out and replaces it with one button, "Restore Suggestion". Submit leaves the card open, showing its status. Dismissed cards do not appear under Past suggestions. Every button gives visible feedback.
10. The click is the practitioner's approval of the product idea, content and confidentiality alike. Nobody tells them they are reviewing. They are never asked to review technical detail.
11. Card order, labels and fixed strings are built into the page, not left to agent instructions.

Confidentiality
12. Three layers: (1) before the card is shown, the scrub removes names and indirect identifiers (unique facts, exact dates, amounts, folder names) to the standard of goal test (b), and the local cross-check runs; (2) the click is the final human gate; (3) the cloud side treats any identifying content found at triage as a leak to purge. The practitioner's withdrawal is a fourth. Nothing is sent that contains a client fact the practitioner didn't see on the card.

Sending and status
13. Status feedback appears on the card only: "Sending...", then "Submitted ✓" or "Not sent, will retry". Errors are plain language, never with an offer of technical details. The raw cause is kept in the local record.
14. At the click, the local server saves the exact payload locally first, then sends. A failed send shows "Not sent" and retries at the next session. Suggestions live in one local store under `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_DATA}`, with no table or column per kind. The local server retries unsent payloads when it starts. After seven failed sessions, the Past suggestions entry reads "Not sent; Probanda could not be reached" and retries stop.
15. Probanda's Cloud knows which Lawyer account sent each suggestion (Account Information).
16. The past-suggestions page shows the local copy word for word, mainly so the practitioner can check nothing confidential was sent. The technical-writer specification is viewable there on request. Derived records (issues, plans, red tests, commits) hold the suggestion id and a paraphrase, never the card's text verbatim; triage reads the card from the cloud record by id. Withdrawal deletes the cloud record and, if triage has begun, closes the issue. The badge reads "Withdrawn".
17. Status badges: Submitted, Under Review, Approved or Declined, and Implemented in <plugin> <version>. Each item (decision 23) has its own status and reason, and the badge summarises them: Submitted until triage opens the record; Under Review while any item is undecided; Declined when every item is declined; Approved when at least one item is approved and not every approved item has shipped, shown as "Approved (n of m)" when some items were declined; Implemented in <plugin> <version> when every approved item has shipped, naming each plugin's release. The card expands to show each item's status and reason. Approved, Declined and Implemented show a reason as visible text, extremely short and readable by a non-technical person; Submitted needs none. A declined item also carries one fixed category, one of which is "not enough detail". Under Review is set when triage opens the record, and an item becomes Implemented when the release that includes a fix commit naming the suggestion id and item number is published. Only Approved and Declined, with the reason, are set by a person. A reason is written for every sender of a merged problem and never quotes submitted text.

Agents
18. `technical-writer` (Opus 5.5, on the practitioner's machine and Claude plan) starts after the click and writes a specification. Practitioners pay for planning from day 1, because changing that later would cost trust. They are told once, at setup or in the terms, that sending a suggestion uses their Claude plan. It reads only the card, the environment facts and the plugins' shipped files under `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}`, never anything under `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_DATA}`, the conversation, documents or tool arguments. A second agent checks the specification as a backstop: it passes when every sentence traces to a line of the card, the environment facts or a shipped plugin file. The specification then goes through the decision 8 check, where a match is removed automatically and logged in the local record, and the practitioner is never prompted about it. The suggestion is sent at the click. The specification and the backstop result follow it to the Supabase suggestion box. If the backstop fails, or a removal leaves the specification incoherent, the suggestion stands alone.
19. Environment facts: for each Probanda plugin, installed or not, with its version; for installed ones, which of Probanda's tools are visible and which expected ones are missing; other tools as a count only; host (Cowork or Code) and OS. Usernames, hostnames and paths are excluded by code, not by instruction. Gathering them must work in both Cowork and Claude Code, without assuming Cowork's tools.
20. `fix-planner` (Opus 5.5, high effort, may propose any approach, read-only on probanda-dev; replaces `/specification-planning`) runs first and returns, for each item, a recommendation with a check that would fail if the pain remained, plus the plan for the items it recommends. It keeps the card's item list: it may recommend part of an item but never splits or merges items. The maintainer approves or declines each item.
21. `fix-maker` (Opus 5.5, medium effort, follows the approved plan's steps without adding to them) carries out only approved items.
22. Briefs are goal-style, with a done-when per agent: card building and scrubbing are done when the full decision 8 check passes with no match and the card is shown; `technical-writer` when the backstop passes; `fix-planner` when each item has a runnable check; `fix-maker` when those checks and the plugin's own checks pass. The Q2 list (where / the pain / what happened / the changes / what counts as wrong / done when) is a reminder line, not a form.

Items, mockups and wording
23. One Suggestion is one card: one shared pain point plus a list of separately approvable change items. Constraints and evidence are context lines with no status. Two unrelated pain points make two cards, shown together.
24. The card offers one "Show a mockup of this" link, below the items, when any item changes how something looks, meaning its layout, colour, typography or page structure. Nothing is built until it is clicked. The mockup page shows all such items together and carries a "Not like this, because..." box. Its text becomes a constraint line (decision 23) and the link comes back, up to three times.
25. The mockup uses only fixed Probanda placeholder text, which holds nothing that matches the date, amount, email or path patterns. The click-time checks run over it.
26. The mockup travels as one self-contained HTML file (inline styles, no scripts, no links, no external resources, under 1 MB): the page the practitioner saw. Only the mockup visible on the card at the click travels. A rejected mockup is deleted locally, and its reason stays as a constraint line.
27. The tool reshapes the practitioner's words into the card's structure but keeps their phrases. Removed identifiers show as visible brackets, each holding only a category from a fixed list: [client], [matter], [person], [document], [file], [date], [amount]. The "our guess, edit if wrong" label is shown per block (the pain point, each item, each context line) when the block holds words the practitioner did not use.
28. The click-time check (decision 8) covers everything sent: pain point, items, comment, raw observation, mockup and environment facts. A match in a part the tool generated (environment facts, raw observation, mockup) removes that line, or for the mockup the whole mockup, automatically and records a defect in the local record; the practitioner is not prompted. The prompt of decision 8 appears only for text the practitioner can read on the card.

Q6 left nothing new: practitioner-started and AI-noticed suggestions differ only by origin cue, raw observation and fingerprint, daily cap and suppression (decisions 2 to 4, 9, 10 and 18).
