# `reformulates` covers both a changed restatement of one principle and a consolidation of several authorities into one changed statement

A court that narrows or broadens a principle and a court that fuses several authorities into one new statement of a test both change the statement of the law. One label is enough to tell a reader that the wording now relied on comes from this authority. See `docs/Specs/2026-10-15_per-principle-treatment-vocabulary-spec.md` §7 Q2.

## Considered options

- **A separate term for consolidation** (the practitioner's earlier "restates"): splits one act, a changed statement of the law, across two labels. "Restates" is also commonly used for a court repeating a test unchanged.

## Consequences

- `reformulates` stays distinct from `explains`, which interprets a statement without changing it.
- A reformulated statement is pushed to the pool as a variant and never rewrites the principle text (legal-commons § Currency).
