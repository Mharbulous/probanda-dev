# Status of the Law Society of BC's cloud and data-location guidance

Follow-up to `2026-10-15_privacy-law-and-data-location.md` (sections "Law Society of BC" and "Unestablished"). That note could reach the Law Society's cloud documents only through search excerpts. This note reads the primary texts directly. Facts only, each with its source. No decision or recommendation is made here. All pages were retrieved on 2026-10-16.

## Question

Is the Law Society of British Columbia's guidance on cloud computing, and on where client records are stored (inside or outside BC or Canada), a binding Law Society Rule or BC Code provision, or a non-binding practice resource? Does any binding provision require records to stay in BC or Canada, or require a lawyer to tell clients that records are stored abroad?

## Short answer

The cloud guidelines and the cloud checklist are practice resources, not Rules. Their advice to tell clients about storage outside BC or Canada is worded as "should", "recommended" and "best practice", never "must". The binding provisions underneath them are Law Society Rules 10-3 (records held with a storage provider) and 10-4 (security of records), the records rules in Part 3 Division 7, and BC Code rule 3.3-1 (confidentiality). None of these requires electronic records to be stored in BC or Canada, and none requires a lawyer to disclose foreign storage to a client. The only location rule found is Rule 3-75(3), which requires *non-electronic* trust accounting records to be kept at the lawyer's chief place of practice in BC for 3 years, and expressly excludes electronic records.

## Findings

### 1. How each cloud document describes its own status

**Cloud computing due diligence guidelines** (Practice Resource DM56523). URL: https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/Website/media/Shared/docs/practice/resources/guidelines-cloud.pdf . Six pages. No date of its own: page 1 says it is "excerpted from the Report of the Cloud Computing Working Group, dated January 27, 2012". The Practice Resources index lists it as "Cloud computing due diligence guidelines (2012)" (https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/practice-resources/).

- Page 1 header: "Practice Resource".
- Page 1, on its privacy part: "The approach in Part B adopts concepts from the Alberta Personal Information Protection Act. It is not prescriptive."
- Page 1 also says "the Law Society views the approach contained in Part B as acceptable" but "the Privacy Commissioner may have a different perspective."
- It mixes "must" and "should". Page 1 opens: "A lawyer must engage in due diligence when using a third party service provider or technology for data storage and/or processing." The trust-record items on page 3 are "best practices" recommended by the Trust Regulation Department.

**Cloud computing checklist v. 4.0** ("Updated January 2023", document numbers DM3804688 and DM2442991). URL: https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/Website/media/Shared/docs/practice/resources/checklist-cloud.pdf . Fourteen pages.

- Page 1: the checklist "is prepared with a view towards raising some of the issues that you should consider prior to moving data into the cloud."
- Page 1: "While the Law Society does not provide a list of approved vendors, the Law Society may declare that a specific entity is not a permitted cloud storage provider (Rule 10-3(5))."
- Page 3, footnote 5, separates the binding part from the rest: "the necessity of complying with Law Society requirements applies to all lawyers", while other parts "may have variable significance to lawyers depending on the nature of their practice."

**Report of the Cloud Computing Working Group**. URL: https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/getContentAsset/b985a3d7-9540-4e46-9664-17fadc93296e/dfc3d011-8f63-43f6-9ed8-4b444333a1d0/CloudComputing.pdf?language=en-CA . Dated "July 15, 2011" on page 1. "Purpose of Report: Discussion and Decision", prepared for the Benchers. It is a policy report recommending guidelines and Rule changes, not a Rule.

- Page 5: the Working Group "believes that what is required is a clear set of practice guidelines to assist lawyers".
- Page 22 (Conclusion): the proper approach "is to provide lawyers due diligence guidelines and best practices."
- Page 23, Recommendation 1: "The Law Society should adopt and publish the attached due diligence guidelines". The guidelines PDF above is that Appendix 1 (pages 27 to 33 of the report), word for word.
- Recommendation 2 (pages 23 to 24) asked for separate Rule changes. Those are what became binding (see section 3).

The report is dated July 15, 2011, but the guidelines PDF calls it "dated January 27, 2012". No source found explains the difference (see Unestablished).

**Practice Resources index page**. URL: https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/practice-resources/ . It lists both cloud documents and says of the resources generally: "Lawyers must exercise professional judgment respecting the correctness and applicability of the material. The Law Society expressly disclaims responsibility for any errors or omissions."

**Advice Decision-Making Assistant, "Cloud computing"**. URL: https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/lsbc/apps/practice-support/adma/concept.cfm?ccid=86 . It lists the binding sources and the guidance separately: "Rules - Records: Law Society Rule 10-3", "Rules - Security of records: Law Society Rule 10-4", and "Practice Resource - More guidance on cloud computing can be found in the practice resource Cloud Computing Checklist."

### 2. The exact words on telling clients about storage outside BC or Canada

Every statement found uses "should", "recommended" or "best practice":

- Guidelines, page 1 (and report, page 27): "If a lawyer uses third party data storage and processing that locates the clients' records outside of British Columbia, the lawyer should advise the client of this fact so the client can determine whether or not to use the lawyer."
- Same place: "It is optimal to memorialize the client's consent in a written retainer."
- Guidelines, page 4, Part B (report, page 31): "If a lawyer is using data storage outside of Canada it is recommended that the lawyer advise the individual at the commencement of the relationship."
- Guidelines, page 5 (report, page 32): "Because of the openness principle, notice should be given to the client that data will be processed outside Canada." Its footnote 6 cites PIPEDA Schedule 1, clause 4.8, not a Law Society Rule.
- Checklist, page 9, Part D 4.c.iii: "The openness principle as incorporated into the Cloud Computing Report by the Law Society makes it a best practice to provide notice to the client that data will be processed and/or stored outside Canada, if applicable."
- Checklist, page 13, Part F 1: "The Law Society's Cloud Computing Report has set out that it is a best practice to receive the informed consent from your client to store their data in the cloud."
- Guidelines, page 3 (Lawyers Insurance Fund steps): "A lawyer should obtain informed client consent for the use of the services".

The "must" statements in Part B are about privacy law, not Law Society rules. Guidelines page 6: "Before or at the time of collecting or transferring personal information to a service provider outside Canada, the lawyer must notify the individual". Its footnote 8 cites the Alberta *Personal Information Protection Act*, ss. 13.1(1) and (2), and page 1 calls Part B "not prescriptive". The only "must" about data location is for public-body data under FIPPA (guidelines page 5): "the lawyer must ensure the information is only stored or accessed within Canada, unless one of the exceptions is met." Footnote 4 cites FIPPA s. 30.1. That restates a statute that applies to a public body's personal information, not a Law Society requirement.

### 3. The binding provisions the guidance rests on, in their own words

The checklist names them. Page 3, footnote 6: "See Law Society Rules 3-60(2), 3-67 to 3-73, 3-75 to 3-76, 3-85 to 3-86, 3-107, 4-55, and 10-3 to 10-4." Page 4, item 12: "Read Law Society Rules 10-3 and 10-4." The text below is quoted from the Rules themselves.

**Rule 10-3, Records** (Part 10, https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/act-rules-and-code/law-society-rules/part-10-%E2%80%93-general/):

- 10-3(1): "'storage provider' means any entity storing or processing records outside of a lawyer's office, whether or not for payment."
- 10-3(2): "a lawyer must, on demand, promptly produce records" in print, read-only access, or an exported "comprehensible format".
- 10-3(4): "A lawyer must not maintain records, including electronic records, with a storage provider unless the lawyer (a) retains custody and control of the records, (b) ensures that ownership of the records does not pass to another party".
- 10-3(4) continues: the lawyer must be "capable of complying with a demand" to produce the records (c), must ensure the provider keeps them securely (d), and must enter "a written agreement with the storage provider that is consistent with the lawyer's obligations" (e).
- 10-3(5): "If the Executive Committee declares, by resolution, that a specific entity is not a permitted storage provider ... no lawyer is permitted to maintain records of any kind with that entity."

**Rule 10-4, Security of records** (same page, "[(1) and (2) amended 05/2021]"):

- 10-4(1): "A lawyer must protect all records related to the lawyer's practice and the information contained in them by making reasonable security arrangements against all risks of loss, destruction and unauthorized access, use or disclosure."
- 10-4(2): the lawyer must "immediately notify the Executive Director in writing" if the lawyer has reason to believe the lawyer "has lost custody or control of any records", or that "anyone has improperly accessed or copied" them.

**Definitions** (https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/act-rules-and-code/law-society-rules/definitions/): "'record' includes metadata associated with an electronic record".

**Part 3, Division 7** (https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/act-rules-and-code/law-society-rules/part-3-%E2%80%93-protection-of-the-public/):

- Rule 3-67(3) allows accounting records in "an electronic form in compliance with subrule (4)". Rule 3-67(4)(a) requires that electronic records be "maintained in a way that will allow compliance with Rule 10-3 (2)".
- Rule 3-75(2): "A lawyer must keep all records ... for at least 10 years from the final accounting transaction or disposition of valuables."
- Rule 3-75(3): "A lawyer must keep records, other than electronic records, at the lawyer's chief place of practice in British Columbia for at least 3 years from the final accounting transaction". Marked "[(2) and (3) amended 05/2021]".
- Rule 3-76(1): the Executive Director "may modify the requirements of that lawyer under Rules 3-68 to 3-71 or 3-75", having regard to the lawyer's "accounting and storage systems".

The 2011 report shows where this came from. In 2011, Rule 3-68(2) required a lawyer to keep records "at his or her chief place of practice in British Columbia" (report, page 11). The report found a cloud user "will likely be off-side this rule by virtue of where the records are stored" (page 11). Recommendation 2.2 (page 23) asked that the "chief place of practice" requirement be removed for electronic records. Current Rule 3-75(3) carries that out by excluding "electronic records". Recommendation 2 (pages 23 to 24) proposed records rules only. None of its nine items proposed a rule on telling clients where records are stored.

**BC Code** (https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/act-rules-and-code/code-of-professional-conduct/chapter-3-%E2%80%93-relationship-to-clients/):

- Rule 3.3-1: "A lawyer at all times must hold in strict confidence all information concerning the business and affairs of a client acquired in the course of the professional relationship and must not divulge any such information unless" authorized by the client, required by law or a court, required to deliver it to the Society, or otherwise permitted.
- 3.3-1 commentary [9]: "it is implied that a lawyer may, unless the client directs otherwise, disclose the client's affairs ... to the extent necessary, to others whose services are used by the lawyer."
- 3.1-2 commentary [4.1]: "A lawyer should understand the benefits and risks associated with relevant technology, recognizing the lawyer's duty to protect confidential information set out in section 3.3".
- Rule 6.1-1 (chapter 6, https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/act-rules-and-code/code-of-professional-conduct/chapter-6-%E2%80%93-relationship-to-students,-employees,-and-others/): "A lawyer has complete professional responsibility for all business entrusted to them". Its commentary deals with supervising non-lawyers and says nothing about technology or storage providers.

The 2011 report (page 13) relied on the predecessor *Professional Conduct Handbook*, Chapter 5, including the duty to "take all reasonable steps to ensure the privacy and safekeeping of a client's confidential information." The current Code chapter 3, read in full for section 3.3, contains no cloud, storage-provider or data-location text.

### 4. Whether any binding provision imposes a data-location rule or a duty to disclose foreign storage

None found. Rules 10-3 and 10-4, Part 3 Division 7, and Code sections 3.1, 3.3 and 6.1 were read for this. Rule 10-3(4) allows records to be kept with a "storage provider" on conditions about custody, ownership, producibility, security and a written agreement. None of those conditions is about where the provider is. Rule 10-3(5) lets the Executive Committee bar a named entity, which is a power over a provider, not a location rule. The one binding location rule, Rule 3-75(3), covers only non-electronic trust accounting records and requires them to be at the chief place of practice in BC. No Rule or Code provision found requires a lawyer to tell a client that records are stored outside BC or Canada. That advice exists only in the practice resources quoted in section 2, plus whatever privacy statute applies to the data.

### 5. How binding force and non-compliance are treated

- **Rules bind, and breach is sanctionable.** *Legal Profession Act*, SBC 1998, c. 9, s. 38(4)(b)(iii) (current to October 6, 2026): after a hearing a panel may determine that the respondent has committed "a breach of this Act or the rules". Source: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/98009_01
- **Records rules are enforced through compliance audits.** Rule 3-85(1): the Executive Director "may at any time order a compliance audit of the books, records and accounts of a lawyer". Rule 3-86(1): a lawyer who does not produce records for the audit "is suspended until the records are produced". Rule 3-49(f) lists as a failure of financial responsibility that "a lawyer does not produce electronic accounting records when required ... in a form required under Rule 10-3 (2)". All in Part 3 at the URL above.
- **The Code states minimum standards, with mandatory and advisory parts.** Introduction, paragraph (3) (https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/act-rules-and-code/code-of-professional-conduct/introduction/): "The BC Code is not a formal part of the Law Society Rules but, rather, an expression of the views of the Benchers about standards that British Columbia lawyers must meet". Paragraph (4): "Each of these components contain some statements that are mandatory, some that are advisory". Also: "Mandatory statements have equal force wherever they appear in the Code."
- **Practice resources disclaim authority.** The index page says lawyers "must exercise professional judgment respecting the correctness and applicability of the material" (quoted in section 1). No statement was found that a practice resource binds lawyers or that departing from one is itself a breach.
- **The report's own view of consequences.** Report, page 22: failure to make technology use comply with professional obligations "may lead to serious legal and regulatory consequences, including revocation or suspension". Recommendation 3 (page 24): a lawyer unable to produce cloud records on demand "may be found to have lost custody or control of the records, which may lead to disciplinary consequences." Both tie consequences to the underlying obligations, not to the guidelines.
- **Rule 10-4 in practice.** The Discipline Advisory of August 31, 2021, "Rule 10-4 Reports" (https://www.lawsociety.bc.ca/for-lawyers/discipline-advisories/august-31,-2021/), restates the duty and asks that a report "explain whether you have informed your clients of the breach". It describes no data-location requirement.

## What binds and what is guidance

| Source | Status | Data location | Telling clients about foreign storage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rule 10-3 (Records) | Binding Rule. Breach is a "breach of ... the rules" (LPA s. 38(4)(b)(iii)) | No location condition. Executive Committee may bar a named provider (10-3(5)) | Not addressed |
| Rule 10-4 (Security of records) | Binding Rule | Not addressed | Not addressed. Duty is to notify the Executive Director of loss or improper access |
| Rule 3-75 (Retention of records) | Binding Rule | Non-electronic trust records at chief place of practice in BC for 3 years. Electronic records excluded | Not addressed |
| BC Code 3.3-1 and commentary | Code. Mandatory statements bind as minimum standards | Not addressed | Not addressed. Commentary [9] implies authority to disclose to service providers "to the extent necessary" |
| Cloud due diligence guidelines (2012, DM56523) | Practice resource. Part B "not prescriptive" | Advises finding out where data is held | "should advise the client" (outside BC). "it is recommended" (outside Canada) |
| Cloud computing checklist v. 4.0 (January 2023) | Practice resource | Asks where servers are located | "best practice" to give notice and to get informed consent |
| Working Group report (July 15, 2011) | Policy report to the Benchers | Recommended removing the chief-place-of-practice rule for electronic records | Recommended the guidelines, not a Rule |

## Unestablished

- Why the guidelines PDF calls the report "dated January 27, 2012" when the report itself is dated July 15, 2011. It may be the date the Benchers adopted it, but no source was found that says so.
- When Rule 10-3(4) and (5) were made. Rule 10-3 on the Part 10 page shows no amendment-history line, and the "Highlights of Amendments" page was not read.
- Whether the Executive Committee has ever made a resolution under Rule 10-3(5) barring any storage provider. No list was found, and the checklist says the Law Society "does not provide a list of approved vendors".
- Whether a Law Society hearing panel has ever found a breach of Rule 10-3 or 10-4, or of Code 3.3-1, based on where records were stored or on failing to tell a client about foreign storage. Discipline decisions were not searched.
- Whether the *Legal Professions Act*, SBC 2024, c. 26, or rules made under it have replaced or will replace the *Legal Profession Act* and these Rules. The BC Laws copy of the *Legal Profession Act* read here is current to October 6, 2026. The newer statute's commencement status was not checked.
- The guidelines and checklist cite rule numbers that predate renumbering. Examples: guidelines page 3 cites "Rule 3-61.1", and the checklist cites "Rule 3-55". These references were not mapped to the current Rules.
- Ethics Committee opinions on third-party service providers (the report's Recommendation 6 asked for them to be updated) were not located or read.
